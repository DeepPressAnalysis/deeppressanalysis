RARE EARTH MAGNETS AND TRANSATLANTIC TUNGSTEN DEPENDENCIES

1. Energy Fuels — Building an Independent Magnet Supply Chain Collides with a Customer Dispute. Within the emerging rare earth magnet supply chain, a structural contradiction has surfaced: government backing and the acquisition of manufacturing assets coincide with uncertainty regarding commercial demand at an already constructed facility.

Observed Actions and Evidence. Energy Fuels agreed to acquire VAC from Ara Partners. The transaction structure announced on June 23, 2026 provides for $718 million in cash consideration and the issuance of 65.853 million shares. Upon closing, Ara is slated to receive approximately 19.9% of Energy Fuels, the right to nominate a director, and specific rights concerning board composition. This arrangement transforms the seller of the magnet business into a major shareholder of the combined raw material and industrial platform (Transaction terms).

However, in Form 8-K dated October 13, 2026, accession 0001062993-26-005306, a different sequence of events was disclosed:

  • in April, a dispute arose between e-VAC Magnetics and a customer;
  • on July 1, the customer delivered a notice of contract termination;
  • on July 7, e-VAC initiated arbitration;
  • on October 13, the parties amended the VAC acquisition agreement, incorporating adjustments linked to the dispute and a potential reduction in the value of the business.

The Sumter facility has a capacity of approximately 2,000 metric tons of magnets per year. Expanding capacity to 12,000 metric tons requires additional capital and new agreements with buyers (Primary SEC document).

Hidden Dependency. Securing proprietary ore, separation capabilities, and metal production does not by itself guarantee capacity utilization at a magnet plant. Its unit economics remain contingent on product qualification by a specific customer, offtake commitments, and contract termination provisions. The causal chain appears to operate as follows: offtake dispute $\rightarrow$ uncertain utilization at Sumter $\rightarrow$ altered expected cash flow $\rightarrow$ adjustment of acquisition price and financing requirements $\rightarrow$ altered pace of expansion across the entire supply chain.

Future Event. Closing the VAC acquisition with Ara emerging among the largest shareholders of Energy Fuels is possible within 180 days, prior to April 19, 2027. The probability is medium: the agreement is signed and is being adapted to identified risks, although regulatory approvals and remaining closing conditions retain significance.

Counter-argument: The existence of a customer termination notice and arbitration does not necessarily preclude closing the transaction or achieving commercial utilization, because the October 13, 2026 contractual amendment explicitly adapts the acquisition structure to the dispute, and a settlement of the arbitration or onboarding of a new long-term buyer could restore the projected utilization trajectory.

Scenario Calculation. At the existing capacity of 2,000 metric tons, illustrative utilization assumptions yield:

Scenario Utilization Assumption Annual Output
Baseline 60% 1,200 t
Favorable 90% 1,800 t
Stress 25% 500 t

This reflects output sensitivity to utilization rather than a company production forecast. The variance between the favorable and stress scenarios is 1,300 metric tons per year without any change in installed capacity.

What Would Alter the Conclusion. Settlement of the arbitration, a new long-term buyer, and confirmed regulatory approvals would strengthen the closing scenario. A repeated price revision, additional financial support from the seller, or a postponement of the transaction would indicate a deepening of the problem.

2. MP Materials — Government Price Protection Creates Financial Resilience While Reinforcing Concentration. Here a model is taking shape in which the state simultaneously supports a company's capital, intermediate product pricing, and future magnet sales.

Observed Actions and Evidence. The July 2025 agreements provided for a $400 million government investment in preferred stock, warrants, a price floor of $110 per kilogram of NdPr, and offtake support for production from the future 10X facility. Initial commissioning of 10X was originally scheduled for 2028 (Public-private partnership terms).

For the second quarter of 2026, MP disclosed:

  • NdPr production of 840 metric tons;
  • income under the price protection agreement of $17.58 million;
  • adjusted EBITDA of $28.493 million;
  • continued qualification deliveries of magnets;
  • Project Swarm to aggregate demand and standardize requirements among unmanned aerial vehicle manufacturers.

Simultaneously, Chinese Announcement No. 23 dated June 22, 2026 placed MP Materials and USA Rare Earth on the export control list. The restriction applies to supplies of Chinese dual-use items to the listed entities, including transfers through third countries; special cases require authorization (MOFCOM document).

Hidden Dependency. U.S. government support enhances the financial resilience of a supplier that simultaneously becomes the target of tailored Chinese restrictions. Consequently, expanding financing may be accompanied by greater complexity in accessing specific materials and technologies. Customer dependency is also present: the three largest clients accounted for 87% of quarterly revenue. This represents sales concentration even if ultimate end-use industries are diverse. The names of anonymized clients in financial reporting cannot be reliably reconstructed solely from revenue shares (Form 10-Q, accession 0001801368-26-000048).

Calculations. Price compensation accounted for 61.7% of quarterly adjusted EBITDA. Mechanically excluding this item leaves $10.913 million. This demonstrates the sensitivity of financial performance to government support under otherwise constant conditions. To evaluate compensation, a simplified relationship is used: $C = Q \times \max(110 - P, 0)$, where $Q$ represents the eligible volume in kilograms of NdPr equivalent and $P$ represents the corresponding price benchmark. Actual calculations depend on contractual definitions of volume and the quarterly benchmark.

Scenario Annual Volume Price Benchmark Illustrative Compensation
Baseline 3,360 t $90/kg $67.2 million
Favorable Market 3,360 t $120/kg $0
Price War 3,000 t $60/kg $150 million

Additionally, 100,000 Monte Carlo runs were performed. Volume was specified via a triangular distribution of 3,000–3,360–4,200 metric tons, and price at $60–$90–$120 per kilogram; the variables were treated as independent. The resulting P10 / median / P90 compensation figures are $12.2 million / $69.9 million / $129.2 million. These bounds reflect the specified assumptions regarding price and volume.

Future Event. Prior to April 19, 2027, disclosure of commercial sales of finished magnets appears probable as qualification progresses. The probability is medium. At the same time, full elimination of production dependency through the 10X facility extends beyond the one-year research horizon.

Counter-argument: Reliance on government price protection payments and high three-customer concentration could diminish if market NdPr benchmark prices rise toward or above $110 per kilogram or if ongoing magnet qualification deliveries and Project Swarm demand aggregation broaden the commercial customer base ahead of the 2028 commissioning of the 10X facility.

Critical Indicators. Revenue specifically generated from finished magnets, completion of qualification, new Project Swarm contracts, customer concentration structure, and actual receipts under the price protection mechanism.

3. Plansee–GTP–Almonty: European Tungsten Diversification Runs Through a U.S. Manufacturing Node. This represents one of the most substantive cross-border dependencies: European financing and European corporate control are combined with processing in the United States and U.S. regulation of secondary raw materials.

Observed Actions and Evidence. Plansee describes the following supply chain:

  • equity participation in Almonty;
  • long-term offtake of output from the Sangdong mine in South Korea;
  • processing of concentrate at Global Tungsten & Powders (GTP) in the United States;
  • subsequent utilization of intermediate products by the group's industrial enterprises.

A release dated October 31, 2025 noted a 15-year offtake agreement and the participation of Austria's Kontrollbank, Germany's KfW, and Plansee in a EUR 75 million financing package (Plansee supply chain disclosure). GTP confirms its ownership by Plansee and manufacturing operations in Towanda, Pennsylvania, including ammonium paratungstate, tungsten oxide, tungsten metal powder, and tungsten carbide (GTP corporate disclosure).

U.S. regulation 2026-16078 (91 FR 50701) introduced, effective August 27, 2026, a requirement to direct 100% of monthly sales of tungsten scrap and specified battery "black mass" to U.S. persons unless the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) grants an exception. The material must remain physically within the United States. The effective period runs through August 27, 2027 (BIS regulation).

New Signal from the Past Seven Days. In a publication on October 15, 2026, Plansee reported that 92% of the tungsten it processes comes from secondary material and endorsed VDMA demands to strengthen European supply. This share applies to the group as a whole; the share of U.S. scrap within its raw material mix was not disclosed.

Hidden Dependency. Reducing reliance on Chinese mining can increase reliance on processing geography, access rights to scrap, and long-term offtake agreements. The U.S. regulatory act applies to specific secondary raw materials; by itself, it does not establish an analogous prohibition on the export of all concentrates or finished tungsten semi-manufactured products.

Future Event. Prior to August 27, 2027, U.S. processors are likely to gain leverage in raw material negotiations alongside a partial reallocation of external orders away from scrap export toward processing inside the United States. The probability is medium; the legal barrier is confirmed, while the scale of restructuring depends on exceptions and actual trade flows.

Counter-argument: Because the U.S. DPAS allocation directive restricts only designated tungsten scrap and battery black mass rather than concentrates or intermediate powders, broad BIS exemptions, increased non-U.S. secondary recycling, or primary concentrate deliveries from the Sangdong mine could prevent any material reduction in feedstock availability for European downstream operations.

Stress Test. For an illustrative processor receiving 20% of its feedstock from the affected U.S. flow:

Scenario Replacement of Restricted Flow Loss of Available Feedstock
Baseline 50% 10%
Favorable 100% 0%
Stress 0% 20%

The 20% exposure is a verification parameter rather than an established share for Plansee.

What Would Falsify the Hypothesis. Broad BIS exceptions, a rapid increase in alternative feedstock, and an absence of shifts in processing geography. Primary confirmations include new processing contracts, export statistics for the relevant scrap categories, and actual deliveries from Sangdong.

NUCLEAR FUEL FINANCING AND SILICON CARBIDE CAPITAL STRUCTURE

4. Centrus — Future Reactor Buyers Become Financing Participants in the Fuel Supply Chain. Vulnerability emerges at the transition between demonstration production and commercial capacity. Capital for expansion is already being allocated, but physical expansion requires several years.

Observed Actions and Evidence. The recipient of the federal task order is American Centrifuge Operating, LLC (UEI L8VHV5CNBV97), a subsidiary of Centrus Energy (CIK 1065059). In USAspending, order 89243226FNE400212 reflects obligations of $900 million, while the total value including all options is $1.07 billion. Contractual obligations cannot be equated to cash funds already disbursed.

On July 1, 2026, Centrus confirmed finalization of the order and reported:

  • completion of production under the prior demonstration contract;
  • transition to a commercial model;
  • expected commissioning of initial new capacity in 2029;
  • initial planned HALEU capacity of approximately 12 metric tons per year.

In August and September, agreements emerged with X-energy, Radiant, and Antares providing for customer prepayments to support enrichment development. The Antares contract is also linked to national security and space programs (X-energy contract disclosure, Antares contract).

Hidden Dependency. A reactor developer becomes simultaneously dependent on fuel availability, the supplier's expansion schedule, and the success of its financing. Buyer prepayments become part of the capital required to establish future supply. The chain of consequences proceeds as follows: new reactor orders $\rightarrow$ prepayments to the fuel supplier $\rightarrow$ construction of enrichment capacity $\rightarrow$ locking in future deliveries $\rightarrow$ concentration of multiple reactor projects around a single manufacturing schedule.

Concentration Calculation. The two announced major Department of Energy (DOE) orders for HALEU—to ACO and General Matter—amount to $900 million each. The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) of the allocation of this $1.8 billion equals 5,000. This reflects concentration of specific funding rather than a measure of market share across operating facilities. A separate $900 million order to Orano pertains to LEU.

Future Event. Prior to April 19, 2027, the emergence of another definitive Centrus agreement regarding supply or buyer-funded expansion appears probable (medium probability). Continued constraints on new industrial capacity through October 2027 are highly probable according to the published schedule.

Counter-argument: Although new enrichment cascades are not scheduled to enter service until 2029, commercial operation of the existing cascade combined with accelerated equipment procurement funded by buyer prepayments could satisfy initial microreactor fuel requirements without delaying early demonstration deployments.

Scenarios. Baseline: commercial operation of the existing cascade and continued construction. Favorable: additional prepayments and accelerated equipment procurement. Stress: delays in transitional agreements or financing that affect the schedules of individual microreactors.

Critical Indicators. Status of commercial operation, Piketon lease terms, actual prepayments, output of new centrifuges, and conversion of conditional backlog into unconditional commitments.

5. Wolfspeed — Government Financing Intersects with Strategic Creditor Rights and European Buyer Interests.

Observed Actions and Evidence. On October 7, 2026, Wolfspeed received a conditional commitment from the Office of Strategic Capital (OSC) to provide up to $1.5 billion over 30 years. The structure contemplates a secured loan disbursed in tranches; the government is slated to receive warrants for an aggregate of up to 7.5% of fully diluted equity, issued proportionally to financing drawdowns. Definitive agreements, creditor consents, and disbursement conditions remain necessary (Commitment terms).

On October 15, eight days later, Wolfspeed and Infineon announced an expansion of their long-term supply agreement to include 200 mm silicon carbide (SiC) wafers.

At the same time, following restructuring, Renesas remains a significant participant in equity and debt instruments. In Schedule 13G/A, accession 0001213900-26-054624, as of March 31, 2026, beneficial ownership of 39.9% was disclosed, accounting for available conversion and an established ownership cap. The voting rights figure in the filing is stated separately and is substantially lower (Primary SEC form).

Hidden Dependency. The resilience of U.S. SiC production is determined by the intersection of three relationships: government credit, post-bankruptcy collateral structure, and commercial demand from strategic buyers. Consequently, aligning financing with existing creditor rights may become the primary near-term constraint.

Future Event. Signing a definitive OSC agreement and receiving the first tranche prior to October 21, 2027 carries a medium probability. The commercial agreement with Infineon strengthens the economic rationale but does not remove contractual lending conditions.

Counter-argument: While post-restructuring creditor rights and collateral covenants present a formal hurdle to loan closing, the expanded 200 mm SiC wafer supply agreement with Infineon strengthens expected cash flows, which may incentivize existing creditors to grant the consents required for phased OSC disbursements.

Financing Scenarios. Baseline: phased financing following approvals. Favorable: access to the entire contemplated $1.5 billion limit. Stress: absence of disbursement, where the announced commitment does not convert into cash inflow. A disbursement of $500 million utilizes one-third of the credit limit and a corresponding share of the contemplated warrant quota; a disbursement of $1 billion utilizes two-thirds. The exact dilution structure depends on final terms.

Critical Indicators. A new Form 8-K containing a definitive credit agreement, amendments to intercreditor agreements, actual receipt of funds, capacity utilization, and execution of Infineon deliveries.

DEFENSE FEEDSTOCKS, MISSILE PROPULSION, AND EUROPEAN MUNITIONS

6. U.S. Antimony: Defense Contract Relies on Foreign Feedstock and Processing.

Observed Actions and Evidence. United States Antimony received a five-year Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) contract with a maximum value of $245 million. In June reporting, individual delivery orders totaling approximately $12 million were disclosed. The first two deliveries—approximately 82,000 pounds, or 37.2 metric tons—were accepted by the government in July, when corresponding revenue of $2.6 million was recognized. The $245 million ceiling is not guaranteed revenue (Form 10-Q, accession 0001104659-26-094035).

In an update on September 30, 2026, the company linked growth in shipments to the receipt of high-purity feedstock from a partner's Bolivian hydrometallurgical enterprise at its Thompson Falls plant (Company release). An additional capital intersection exists: in October 2025, US Antimony acquired approximately 10% of Australia's Larvotto Resources. The company accounts for this position as an investment and does not claim significant influence through management.

Hidden Dependency. The U.S. origin of the final defense delivery is combined with an international chain of raw materials, technology, and investment interests. Causal chain: DLA order $\rightarrow$ need for suitable feedstock $\rightarrow$ foreign hydrometallurgy $\rightarrow$ U.S. finishing processing $\rightarrow$ government acceptance $\rightarrow$ revenue recognition.

Future Event. Continuation of newly accepted DLA deliveries prior to January 19, 2027 carries a high probability based on the active order and the September feedstock update. Retention of foreign links in the supply chain over a one-year horizon also appears probable. The major Perpetua Stibnite project in Idaho is oriented toward production in 2029 and must not be conflated with Stibnite Hill in Montana, which appears in US Antimony releases. Financing future mining does not cover the current transitional period.

Counter-argument: Although current DLA fulfillment depends on foreign hydrometallurgical feedstock and ties up working capital, expanding domestic raw material extraction from Montana's Stibnite Hill could progressively substitute imported inputs and lower working capital intensity well before Idaho's Stibnite project enters production in 2029.

Calculation and Scenarios. US Antimony's antimony inventories in monetary terms grew from $12.02 million to $21.38 million, or by 77.9%. This is an indicator of tied-up working capital that depends on both physical quantity and material cost. Baseline scenario: feedstock receipts and acceptance proceed sequentially. Favorable: growth in domestic feedstock reduces costs. Stress: import or acceptance delays increase working capital requirements.

Critical Indicators. Volumes of DLA-accepted product, new orders within the framework contract, inventory turnover, Bolivian feedstock deliveries, and operating margin.

7. L3Harris–AXYV: Direct Government Supplier Financing Reshapes Risk Distribution in the Missile Industry.

Observed Actions and Evidence. In April 2026, a $1 billion government investment in Missile Solutions closed. The funds are designated for production expansion, including facilities serving multiple missile programs (Closing release).

In Form 10-Q dated July 30, accession 0000202058-26-000058, material terms of the preferred instrument were disclosed:

  • a liquidation preference accruing at 7% per annum with quarterly compounding;
  • conversion upon a qualifying IPO prior to the end of 2027;
  • a separate redemption mechanism in 2031 if a qualifying IPO does not take place.

These pathways depend on contractual conditions (Note B to the financial statements). The initial target of an IPO in the second half of 2026 has already been revised: on the July earnings call, management reported an intention to return to the matter in mid-2027. Simultaneously, negotiations regarding new contracts exceeding $20 billion were discussed; these are negotiations rather than signed backlog.

Hidden Dependency. The state finances a manufacturing link that serves multiple end programs. The risk of delay propagates simultaneously across different systems, while the rights of the government investor exist separately from the rights of future common shareholders.

Calculation. For the applicable preference accrual branch: $1,000 \times (1 + 0.07 / 4)^4 = \$1,071.86\text{ million}$. Over four quarters, the increase amounts to $71.86 million; over two quarters, $35.31 million. This reflects the contractual sensitivity of the value of the corresponding claim rather than a mandatory cash payment under every developmental pathway.

Future Event. New definitive long-term orders and continued expansion through October 2027 carry a medium-to-high probability. The absence of an IPO prior to April 19, 2027 carries a high probability given the latest public guidance.

Counter-argument: If negotiations covering more than $20 billion in prospective missile contracts convert rapidly into binding multi-year backlog, improved revenue visibility could support an earlier public offering window in 2027 and trigger automatic conversion of the preferred instrument prior to the end-of-2027 threshold.

Scenarios. Baseline: construction continues prior to revisiting the IPO. Favorable: contracts lock in utilization, facilitating the offering. Stress: the offering is postponed again while expansion requires additional financing.

Indicators. Signed orders replacing frameworks, public registration terms for AXYV, commissioning of new sites, and dynamics of contract prepayments.

NOVEMBER REGULATORY CROSSROADS, SIGNAL VALIDATION, AND EVENT MATRIX

Immediate Regulatory Crossroads: November Dates Require Separate Reading. U.S. regulation 2025-19846 provides for the suspension of the BIS Affiliates Rule to end on November 9, 2026, with requirements returning on November 10. The original rule extends applicable restrictions to entities with 50% or greater direct or indirect ownership by listed persons, including through share aggregation (Regulation text).

Chinese Announcement No. 70 dated November 7, 2025 suspended specified October measures until November 10, 2026. However, on September 28, 2026, MOFCOM reported an extension of broader arrangements until January 10, 2027. This is a substantive signal in favor of a further deferral; exact applicability must be established through separate implementing acts.

Therefore, over a 30-day horizon through November 20, the baseline scenario is clarification or extension of specific regimes. The stress scenario is a resumption of partial requirements due to mismatched legal deadlines. The favorable scenario is a coordinated formalization of extensions that reduces supply uncertainty. The probability of simultaneous full tightening by both sides is not reliably established; the September agreement substantially weakens such a forecast.

Counter-argument: Even though the September 28, 2026 MOFCOM statement points toward an extension through January 10, 2027, the statutory expiration dates of November 9 and November 10, 2026 remain legally binding until separate implementing acts are formally promulgated, leaving a narrow window in which procedural delays could trigger temporary enforcement.

Historical Verification: Which Signals Produce False Conclusions.

Institutional Ownership. Eight quarterly snapshots of BlackRock's position in MP Materials from September 2024 through June 2026 were verified. For the five periods when the value of the holding grew, the results are as follows:

Underlying positions are aggregated by CUSIP 553368101 from Form 13F information tables (Primary filing for Q1 2026, and for Q2 2026). Within this small sample, the rule "holding value rises $\rightarrow$ the fund is accumulating shares" was wrong in four out of five cases, or 80%. This is a test of a specific accumulation indicator rather than the error rate of all event forecasts. For temporal causality, another factor is also material: a quarterly position does not show the exact date of purchase. For example, data as of June 30 published in August do not prove trading immediately prior to a July government decision. The complete mandatory Form 13F picture for Q3 2026 appears only by November 16. Therefore, October institutional positioning cannot currently be reliably reconstructed from a complete set of quarterly disclosures.

Insider Transactions. An MP Materials Form 4 dated October 13 reflects the withholding of 338 shares for David Infuso to pay taxes upon receipt of compensation, transaction code F. This is not an open-market sale based on an investment decision (Primary Form 4). The verified transaction does not strengthen the hypothesis of an insider warning.

Government Support and Financial Resilience. In October 2024, Wolfspeed received preliminary terms for proposed CHIPS financing of up to $750 million. In June 2025, bankruptcy followed. Historical conclusion: strategic importance and preliminary support do not guarantee preservation of a prior capital structure.

Government Capital and Offering Timelines. At L3Harris, the announcement of an investment and the confidential submission of IPO registration documents did not lock in the initial offering timeline: the target was shifted to 2027. Industrial demand and public market readiness to value an asset evolve with different lags.

These verifications make it possible to filter out several common false signals. They do not provide a sufficient sample to calibrate numerical probabilities across eight different corporate events. Therefore, probabilities below are expressed qualitatively, while quantitative results pertain to conditional consequences.

Counter-argument: Although passive 13F market value changes, tax-related Form 4 withholdings, and non-binding government term sheets frequently generate false positives when read in isolation, cross-referencing binding Form 8-K and Form 10-Q contractual disclosures with physical delivery and regulatory records still provides a reliable basis for qualitative event forecasting.

SYSTEMIC INTERSECTIONS & CAUSAL HYPOTHESES

SYNTHESIS • DOCUMENTARY SOURCES
05

CRITICAL INTERSECTIONS, CAUSAL HYPOTHESES, AND DOCUMENTARY SOURCES

Most Critical Intersections.

Government Financing and Corporate Control. In MP, Wolfspeed, and Missile Solutions, the state receives differing combinations of preferred rights, warrants, and contractual commitments. Economic influence here is determined by claim seniority, disbursement conditions, and offtake guarantees. The percentage of common shares reflects only part of the relationship.

Institutional Capital and Industrial Positioning. The most substantive shifts were identified in strategic investments, acquisition terms, and buyer prepayments: Ara–Energy Fuels, Renesas–Wolfspeed, and fuel buyers–Centrus. Growth in the dollar value of a passive stake provides substantially weaker evidence of a new strategic decision.

Export Restrictions and Production Dependencies. MP simultaneously receives U.S. backing and a targeted Chinese restriction. Plansee simultaneously builds an alternative to Chinese raw materials and utilizes U.S. processing. Diversification shifts the location of the dependency and the set of applicable rules.

Government Financing and Commercial Demand. VAC demonstrates the risk of idle or underutilized capacity during a dispute with a buyer. Centrus demonstrates the reverse side: the buyer must participate in financing the supplier to secure future product availability.

Corporate Ownership and Visible Competition. Multiple legal entities, countries, and projects may belong to a single group or joint structure. Procurement and grant concentration must be calculated simultaneously across legal recipients, corporate groups, and physical manufacturing sites.

Cascading Consequences. The most acute combination arises when multiple end programs depend on a single intermediate process requiring lengthy qualification: magnets, enrichment, propelling charges, or specialized semiconductors. Financial support may appear substantially earlier than incremental industrial output.

The evidentiary base is most complete for the examined U.S. companies and linked European supply chains. A continuous comparable panel of all SAM/TED contracts, subcontracts, NPORT filings, and insider transactions has not been formed; conclusions regarding concentration apply to the explicitly cited contracts, projects, and disclosures.

Counter-argument: Because the evidentiary sample focuses on disclosed U.S. and European bottlenecks rather than a comprehensive global panel of all subcontracts and private facilities, unobserved secondary suppliers or parallel commercial capacity expansions outside the cited disclosures could reduce systemic concentration over a multi-year timeframe.

New Causal Hypotheses for Further Monitoring.

  1. State-backed suppliers will more frequently become targets of tailored external restrictions. Verification: alignment of new government investments with subsequent additions to export control lists.
  2. A commercial offtake contract may prove to be a more binding constraint than ore availability. Verification: VAC capacity utilization, arbitration outcome, and capital expenditures required to transition to new buyers.
  3. Buyers will more actively finance critical suppliers through prepayments. Verification: new Centrus agreements and disclosed customer commitments to finance capacity.
  4. Control over secondary raw materials will become an independent instrument of industrial leverage. Verification: expansion of U.S. allocation requirements to other materials and shifts in processing locations.
  5. Financial improvement at strategic companies will precede the physical elimination of supply dependency. Verification: ratio of price compensations, prepayments, and revaluations to the output of qualified product.
  6. Concentration will migrate from mining assets to qualified intermediate manufacturing nodes. Primary monitored nodes: Sumter, Mountain Pass and Independence, Piketon, Towanda, Missile Solutions manufacturing sites, and Nitrochemie Aschau.

Counter-argument: If downstream buyers standardize component specifications—such as through UAV demand aggregation under Project Swarm—or if multi-source qualification accelerates across intermediate nodes, bargaining power and concentration at individual processing facilities could moderate even before long-horizon mining and enrichment projects reach full capacity.
