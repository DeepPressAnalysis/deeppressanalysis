THE ARCHITECTURE OF TRANSNATIONAL CONTROL: EUROCLEAR

The Euroclear corporation represents a fundamental node of transnational financial infrastructure, controlling the settlement, safekeeping, and servicing of securities on a global scale. Its architecture is built not on the model of a classical commercial enterprise, but on the principle of a quasi-sovereign infrastructural monopoly that dictates the conditions for accessing global liquidity.

1.1. Key Corporate Strategy Type

Primary type: Financial Architect. Euroclear forms and retains power through total control over the architecture of capital movement, clearing, and access to liquidity. Unlike traditional banking conglomerates, the corporation does not own the underlying physical assets, yet it entirely controls the infrastructure for their accounting, legitimation, and transfer. By the end of 2024, the volume of Assets Under Custody (AUC) on the group's balance sheet reached a historic record of €40.7 trillion, with the system processing transactions exceeding €1 quadrillion annually. Such unprecedented concentration makes Euroclear the exclusive operator of systemic financial risk on the continent. Disconnecting any market or sovereign counterparty from its systems automatically implies de facto financial isolation and technical default on external obligations, as clearly demonstrated by the complete blockade of the assets of the National Settlement Depository (NSD) of the Russian Federation in March 2022.

Secondary type: Gatekeeper. The corporation has de facto monopolized the function of connecting isolated national markets, serving as an unalterable bridge between issuers and investors. Operating settlements in 51 currencies and providing direct access to the domestic markets of 48 countries, Euroclear capitalizes on its gatekeeper role. National states critically depend on the Euroclear platform for placing sovereign debt and ensuring its liquidity. The institutional power of the corporation is based on the fact that no sovereign issuer outside the US financial system can effectively manage its debt without direct or indirect connection to its gateways.

However, a counter-example to classic "gatekeeper" behavior is Euroclear's conscious rejection of infrastructural neutrality in modern geopolitical conflicts. Unlike the traditional model of 20th-century Swiss banking, the corporation rigidly and proactively implements sanctions regimes, acting as a conduit for the geopolitical will of EU and US institutions. Restructuring processes to retain blocked Russian assets and seize revenues from them shifts the structure's functionality from a passive financial gateway to an active financial architect employing sanctions coercion.

1.2. Decision-Making Model

The real decision-making center within the corporation is a complex system of checks and balances, which has historically shifted from an initial consortium of users (brokers and investment banks) to a closed network of quasi-state and institutional investors integrated with the Board of Directors of Euroclear Holding SA/NV. The decision-making model can be classified as a combination of state curatorship and institutional control.

The corporation's Board of Directors forms policy in which immediate commercial interests are subordinated to the long-term objectives of national (Belgian) and pan-European financial stability. Between 2022 and 2024, a fundamental transformation of the control structure occurred: traditional commercial participants began to yield to sovereign capital. For example, Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) and BNP Paribas sold their shares to the state government funds of Belgium (SFPI-FPIM) and France (Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, CDC). The participation of public sector representatives on the Board of Directors (such as Philippe Mills from CDC and Bart De Smet from SFPI) guarantees that the corporation's strategic maneuvers are synchronized with the fiscal and geopolitical interests of the Western bloc.

The institutional reform completed on October 1, 2024, when the intermediate holding structures Euroclear AG and Euroclear Investments SA/NV were absorbed by Euroclear Holding SA/NV, demonstrates a rigid centralization of the management contour. Decisions are made by a consolidated group of sovereign shareholders with direct lobbying access to EU regulators. This allows the corporation to rapidly adapt the external legal framework to its needs—for instance, the speed of drafting and adopting EU Regulations 2024/576 and 2024/1469 on the use of "windfall profits" was entirely synchronized with Euroclear's corporate tax payment schedule and dividend cycles.

1.3. Approach to Regulatory and Political Risk

The corporation's interaction with the regulatory environment is built on a paradoxical principle: Euroclear uses the publicity of its status as a bearer of systemic risk (FMI — Financial Market Infrastructure) as an absolute defensive shield against regulatory pressure and external expropriation. The corporation lobbies for changes in international and European rules exclusively for its own operational needs, simultaneously buying a piece of national sovereignty through direct tax contributions.

First, when the European Commission and the US seriously considered the full confiscation of frozen sovereign assets of the Central Bank of the Russian Federation (totaling over €190 billion on Euroclear's balance sheet) in 2023–2025, the corporation, joining forces with the Belgian government and the European Central Bank (ECB), declared the risk of a collapse in global trust in the euro and the threat of a massive exodus of sovereign investors from the Global South—a so-called "Euroclear run". This ultimatum blocked the direct confiscation of the principal reserve amount, forcing the coalition to limit itself only to the seizure of interest income. The threat of its own insolvency was used as an instrument to dictate terms to the European Council.

Second, Euroclear successfully lobbies for the transformation of European law to legalize its sanctions income. The implementation of EU Regulations 2024/576 and 2024/1469 permitted the legalization of seizing net windfall profits from the CBR assets, but the rules were written in such a way that Euroclear is officially allowed to retain 0.3% of the net profit, as well as deduct corporate tax and expenses for managing legal risks before transferring the funds to the European Peace Facility.

Third, the corporation literally buys sovereign protection through tax contributions. Windfall profits from frozen Russian assets in 2024 alone brought the Belgian state budget an unprecedented €1.7 billion in corporate tax. These injections made the Belgian government the chief advocate and political shield for the corporation at European Council meetings, fiercely blocking any initiatives capable of damaging the depository's balance sheet.

1.4. Main Internal Driver

Abstracting from official declarations about "protecting market integrity," the dominant motive of the system is rent-seeking control over pools of captured liquidity and a monopoly on settlement standards. Euroclear does not simply provide clearing services; the corporation forms an unalterable transaction channel. Under conditions of macroeconomic or geopolitical crises, this driver takes a radical form: the corporation extracts gigantic rent simply from the fact that foreign capital is blocked.

For 2023, net interest income from Russian assets blocked in Euroclear Bank accounts amounted to €4.4 billion. For 2024, the total amount of interest income from these assets reached nearly €6.9 billion. In the first quarter of 2026, despite the onset of fund transfers to the EU, interest income continued to grow due to high deposit volumes. Thus, the key internal driver becomes retaining architectural control over passive liquidity in an environment of high ECB interest rates, allowing the generation of billions in revenues without conducting active investment or operational activities in the traditional sense.

The initial banking leverage evolved into a supranational institution whose architecture was originally built to service transnational offshore capital (Eurobonds), independent of direct control by national governments of the US or individual European countries.

KEY ACTIONS AND MARKET POSITIONING

Between 2023 and 2026, the corporation made a series of institutional decisions that radically altered the contours of global financial law.

3.1. Where the Corporation Went Against the System

Blocking the full confiscation of Russian sovereign assets (2023–2025): Amidst colossal pressure from the US (up to the passing of the REPO Act) and Eastern European countries demanding the confiscation of €190+ billion in CBR reserves to transfer to Ukraine, Euroclear (relying on support from Belgium, Germany, and the ECB) went against the geopolitical mainstream. The corporation argued that direct confiscation of the asset principal would violate sovereign immunity, provoke a massive outflow of Global South capital from the European jurisdiction, and lead to the depository's bankruptcy due to inevitable counterclaims in Asia and the Middle East.

Consequence: The system capitulated to the infrastructural monopoly. In December 2025, the European Council finally abandoned the plan for a direct "Reparation Loan" backed by the principal of Russian assets themselves, replacing it with a standard EU macro-financial loan of €90 billion. Euroclear successfully defended its balance sheet, proving that its institutional stability is more important to the EU than the geopolitical goals of its allies. The proponents of radical sanctions lost; Euroclear won, preserving the status quo.

3.2. Where It Integrated into the System

Windfall Profits Mechanism and ERA Loans (2024–2025): When pressure on the corporation became critical, Euroclear agreed to a compromise, deeply embedding itself in the state financing functions. As blocked CBR bonds mature, the funds are converted into cash and settle on Euroclear's balance sheet. The bank's balance sheet grew from €25 billion in 2021 to €230 billion in March 2026. The corporation reinvests this cash, extracting immense interest income (about €6.9 billion in 2024).

The European Commission legalized this flow, obliging Euroclear to transfer 99.7% of its net profit to European funds for Ukraine (European Peace Facility and Ukraine Facility). Euroclear transferred €1.55 billion in July 2024, €1.6 billion in July 2025, and reserved €1.4 billion for early 2026.

Consequence: Euroclear de facto turned into a sovereign donor, embedded in the macro-financial support of a foreign state. The corporation transformed the risk of expropriation into a managed tax, retaining the principal assets and 0.3% of the net windfall profit plus expense compensation.

3.3. Where It Defied Expectations

Asymmetric compensations via "asset swaps" (March 2024 — 2025): While EU politicians declared an uncompromising sanctions war, Euroclear quietly executed a legal maneuver to protect its own systemic clients. In March 2025, the Belgian regulator granted Euroclear permission to unblock a portion of funds from NSD accounts. These funds were used to compensate the losses of Western banks and corporate clients affected by Russian counter-sanctions (specifically, by RF court decisions under Presidential Decree No. 198).

Consequence: The corporation defied the expectations of radical sanctions supporters, de facto launching an asymmetric and behind-the-scenes "asset swap" between the RF and the EU to protect its own client network. This precedent proves that the institutional interest in retaining the loyalty of Western banks is more important to Euroclear than observing a total sanctions embargo.

3.4. Real Contributions and Innovations

Outside the political context, Euroclear's power is based on genuine infrastructural innovations:

  • DVP Mechanism (Delivery-Versus-Payment): The introduction of simultaneous asset delivery versus payment in commercial and central bank money eliminated historical counterparty risk, becoming the absolute global standard for settlements.
  • Global Collateral Pool (Collateral Highway): Euroclear created an architecture enabling financial institutions to automatically mobilize assets worldwide to secure margin trades and meet Basel III requirements. This tool created technological dependence of dealer banks on Euroclear platforms.
  • Fund Platformization (Euroclear FundsPlace): The aggregation of mutual and investment fund accounting into a single ecosystem, which solidified the corporation's dominance in the asset management market (up to €3.4 trillion in the funds segment).

The legal crisis concerning Russian assets revealed a deep structural feature of the model: Euroclear's jurisdictional armor is impenetrable within the Western legal system, but it provokes the fragmentation of global markets. A precedent has been set in which sovereign reserves can not only be frozen but also subjected to economic depletion (seizure of revenues). For competitors and Global South nations, this is a signal of the necessity to create alternative clearing systems (within BRICS) that are not controlled by EU institutions.
