BIOPOLITICS • ALGORITHMIC CONTROL
01

THE BIOPOLITICS OF ATTENTION: THE TIKTOK CASE

A coalition of fourteen US states and the District of Columbia has initiated lawsuits against the TikTok platform, accusing the corporation of implementing algorithms intentionally designed to induce psychological addiction in minor users1. According to internal company documents made public during the litigation, TikTok conducted algorithmic A/B testing in which a control group of fifteen million American users was intentionally stripped of protective mechanisms designed to prevent the formation of "information bubbles"2. This test, aimed at evaluating the impact of safety filters on key engagement metrics (DAU), resulted in the accounts of vulnerable teenagers—including Chase Nasca, who died by suicide—being continuously exposed to depressive and suicidal content2.

Simultaneously, the European Commission preliminarily concluded that the application's architecture violates the Digital Services Act (DSA) regarding addictive design4. Meanwhile, the US Supreme Court upheld the constitutionality of the law forcing the divestiture of ByteDance assets, citing national security threats, while completely disregarding arguments concerning potential content manipulation5.

Narrative Vector: The text array strictly directs the reader's perception toward amplifying the legitimacy of the repressive state apparatus. Technological corporations, particularly those with foreign capital, are constructed as an existential threat operating with cynical calculation, prioritizing financial metrics over human life. The younger generation is entirely stripped of subjectivity and agency, portrayed exclusively as passive subjects of neurobiological manipulation. The state and the judicial system are positioned as the sole rational actors possessing the moral and legal authority to intervene in the architecture of private software.

Rhetorical Devices: The texts employ active emotional anchoring through terminology that criminalizes corporate practices. A/B testing is routinely described as a "depraved experiment," and the platform itself is likened to an "unlicensed virtual economy" and a "virtual strip club"1. An expert smokescreen is utilized, wherein medical lexicon ("dopamine reward," "dysmorphia") legitimizes legal bans. A normalization of the extraordinary occurs: judicial orders mandating the alteration of base machine code are presented not as censorship, but as acts of sanitary control.

Substituting Fact with Emotion: A technical failure of the moderation system or a cynical corporate optimization of metrics is replaced by a deeply personalized tragedy of a specific teenager. The texts do not provide a detailed analysis of how recommendation graphs function or the role of the social environment in adolescent depression; instead, they generate a sustained state of moral panic, parental helplessness, and a sharp demand for immediate retribution, effectively blocking any cold structural analysis of the problem2.

PHARMACOLOGICAL CAPITALISM

MEDICALIZATION • SOCIAL ENGINEERING
02

BIOPOLITICS AND PHARMACOLOGICAL CAPITALISM: THE GLP-1 CASE

In the United States, approximately thirty-three thousand prescriptions for GLP-1 receptor agonist medications have been issued to patients under the age of eighteen9. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of clinical trials demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in Body Mass Index Z-score (Z-BMI) and improvements in cardiometabolic parameters among children and adolescents. For instance, in the 6-12 age group, the median Z-BMI decreased from +3.90 to +3.06, with over eighty percent of patients achieving a BMI reduction of 5% or more9. Concurrently, a rapid surge in prescriptions is documented, stimulated not only by clinical indications (rising incidence of type 2 diabetes) but also by the pressure of social networks forming new aggressive standards of corporeality12.

Narrative Vector: This text array redirects perception toward the absolute legitimation of the pharmaceutical establishment. The human body, unmodified by chemistry, is weakened and stigmatized as defective. Generations Alpha and Z are transformed into objects of permanent, lifelong medical administration. Science and pharmacology are constructed as the rational subjects, while natural biological fluctuations and sociocultural standards are positioned as insurmountable problems.

Rhetorical Devices: The key instrument here is statistical obfuscation: rigorous clinical data combating severe obesity and diabetes are seamlessly intertwined with consumer, cosmetic usage of the drugs aimed at achieving social standards. A false dilemma is applied, wherein ineffective individual efforts (diets, exercise) are contrasted against effective injections, completely excluding the structural causes of obesity (food policy, urban ecology) from the discourse12. The appeal to inevitability normalizes the prospect of multi-decade pharmacological intervention.

Substituting Fact with Emotion: Instead of a cold analysis of endocrine mechanisms and long-term risks during puberty, the texts generate a state of social urgency and fear of stigmatization. The depiction of flawless bodies on social networks and peer pressure induces deep guilt in readers (primarily parents) regarding their children's appearance and anxiety over their future social status, which directly converts into a willingness to pay for expensive therapy.

Hidden Assumptions: Unspoken axioms lie at the foundation of these texts: the body is viewed as a malfunctioning mechanism requiring exogenous calibration. It is accepted as a given that algorithmic standards of beauty are insurmountable, implying that adolescent biology must adapt, rather than the structure of society. A lifelong dependence on expensive medication is implicitly presented as an acceptable, rational price for social integration.

SOVEREIGNTY • MACROECONOMICS
03

ECONOMIC DETERMINISM AND THE LIMITS OF POLITICAL IMAGINATION: THE QUEBEC CASE

The Parti Québécois, leading in pre-election polls, declares its intention to hold a referendum on the province's independence from Canada. Estimates of the public debt for a potentially independent Quebec diverge radically: sovereigntists estimate it at $397 billion (including a $185 billion share of the federal debt), arguing this yields an improved debt-to-GDP ratio and $8.8 billion in savings from eliminating duplicative functions14. Opponents and federal institutions (McGill University, HEC Montréal) point to a debt burden of 92% of GDP, comparable to crisis-stricken European nations15. Secession would entail the loss of annual federal transfers (equalization payments) ranging from $9.6 billion to $13 billion16. The provincial finance bloc notes that political uncertainty itself has already increased borrowing costs by 5 basis points, equivalent to an annual loss of $20 million18.

Narrative Vector: The text array strictly channels perception into the realm of absolute economic pragmatism. Macroeconomic institutions, rating agencies, and federal power structures are amplified, serving as the arbiters of reality. Separatist political forces are constructed as bearers of irrational, mathematically unsound, and potentially destructive ideas. The global financial market (the bond market) is positioned as the supreme judge of political processes, depriving the nation of the right to make a historical choice outside of accounting calculations.

Rhetorical Devices: Future blackmail dominates: the reader is presented with scenarios involving a catastrophic GDP collapse, capital flight by corporations, and an inability to finance the social sector19. The devaluation of alternatives is achieved by reducing complex issues of cultural and linguistic identity to disputes over basis points and borrowing costs18. An expert smokescreen is widely applied, where the analytics of economic institutions translate an unresolvable political conflict into the format of a dry audit, masking ideological bias15.

Hidden Assumptions: The texts are predicated on the assumption that economic discipline and credit ratings possess unconditional priority over a nation's rights. Sovereignty is viewed exclusively as a complex financial transaction, rather than a historical process. It is implicitly asserted that no state in the modern world can survive if its actions provoke the displeasure of the global sovereign bond market18.

CULTURE • COMMODIFICATION
04

COMMODIFICATION OF IDENTITY IN THE ATTENTION ECONOMY

Summer box office revenues for Hollywood releases in 2026 reached $4.6 billion, 26% higher than the same period previously, restoring pre-pandemic levels22. The largest contributions came from original productions and Imax formats (Imax network revenue hit a record $257 million in July, with shares soaring 38%)23. The film The Odyssey grossed $1.6 billion, while Spider-Man: Brand New Day pulled in $2.34 billion22. Simultaneously, the entertainment industry is documenting explosive growth in the short vertical series (micro-drama) market, with non-Asian revenue estimated at $3 billion (led by ReelShort and DramaBox)25. The technological startup Suno AI, which generates music via text prompts, reached a $5 billion valuation despite massive copyright infringement lawsuits from major labels26. In the consumer goods sector, the e.l.f. Beauty brand achieved a market capitalization of $6.34 billion29.

Narrative Vector: The narrative stream amplifies the legitimacy of corporations controlling intellectual property and digital attention aggregators. The consumer is constructed as a predictable subject of permanent administration, whose cognitive deficits (loneliness, boredom, the search for authenticity) are algorithmically monetized. Producers, CEOs, and algorithms act as the rational subjects, successfully constructing patterns to retain user attention. Traditional culture is weakened, giving way to content optimized for micro-consumption.

Substituting Fact with Emotion: Instead of a structural analysis detailing the causes of mass social escapism, the texts romanticize and gamify the consumption process. Enthusiastic financial reports and market success stories create a sense of participation in the global triumph of the industry, masking the total alienation of the individual from reality and their immersion in surrogate emotional experiences generated by neural networks and producers.

Hidden Assumptions: The texts harbor the presumption that human attention is an freely extractable resource (harvesting) that must be monetized down to the last second. Escapism is acknowledged as the inevitable consequence of a complex reality. It is implicitly asserted that any form of identity can only be realized through the act of purchasing the corresponding goods or subscribing to content25.

TECHNO-NATIONALISM

GEOECONOMICS • SEMICONDUCTORS
05

GEOECONOMICS AND THE SEMICONDUCTOR BATTLEFIELD

The Chinese corporation ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) has achieved fourth place globally in memory chip (DRAM) production, capturing 7.67% of the global market. The company's revenue in the first quarter of 2026 hit 50.8 billion yuan (approximately 31% of the revenue of its American competitor, Micron)31. These metrics demonstrate the efficacy of China's state policy of import substitution and the creation of a closed ecosystem in response to export restrictions and technological sanctions imposed by the US and its allies.

Narrative Vector: The narrative rigidly reinforces the concept of the sovereign technological state. China is presented as a highly rational, strategically thinking subject capable of long-term planning. The sanction policies of Western nations are weakened in the discursive field, positioned not as barriers, but as potent catalysts enabling the adversary to achieve technological autarky. The myth of the global free market is shattered; reality is constructed as an uncompromising struggle between national industrial and military-technological complexes.

Rhetorical Devices: The appeal to inevitability is widely utilized: CXMT's breakthrough successes are presented as the logical outcome of state capitalism. A normalization of the extraordinary takes place, wherein the fracturing of global supply chains and a cold war between technological blocs are framed as the natural, baseline state of the new world order. Emotional anchoring is employed through terms like industrial survival and technological sovereignty.

Hidden Assumptions: A hidden axiomatic assertion permeates the texts: the era of globalization is over, and the future belongs to autarkic blocs. State subsidization (industrial policy) is viewed as the only viable mechanism for technological leadership in times of crisis. National security is directly and indisputably equated with control over semiconductor manufacturing.

INSTITUTIONAL SYNTHESIS

NARRATIVE ANALYSIS • POWER ARCHITECTURE
06

CROSS-MEDIA SYNTHESIS AND THE ARCHITECTURE OF POWER

The Authorial Mindset (Model of the World)
In the institutional author's view, the world operates as a colossal, multi-tiered administrative-market system trapped in a state of permanent polycrisis (geopolitical, mental, economic). In this logic, phenomena, ethics, and cultural artifacts simply do not exist outside of market valuation or security parameters. The world is a minefield of risks demanding continuous institutional governance. Problems are never resolved through deep structural societal changes, but exclusively through the application of technological, pharmacological, legal, or financial "patches" (algorithmic bans, obesity injections, interest rate hikes).

The Attribution of Rationality
Rationality, subjectivity, and the right to act are vested exclusively in the elites. This includes tech CEOs, architects of national industrial policy, investors, and central bankers. Their actions, even when leading to social tragedy, monopolization, or censorship, are always described using the language of strategy, competence, and objective economic determinism. The state acts as the absolute rationalizer of chaos4. Conversely, society at large—from teenagers to average voters—is entirely stripped of subjectivity. They are framed as passive reservoirs of attention, data, and capital, driven purely by dopamine, irrational fears, or an infantile need for social approval1.

The Illusion of Inevitability
The industrialization of the technological race ("the state is forced to protect chip production"), the medicalization of puberty ("teenagers will inevitably take drugs"), the algorithmization of content ("micro-dramas are objectively what viewers demand"), and the suppression of separatism ("bond markets dictate the rules")—these are all profoundly political, ideological, and corporate choices. Yet, the language of the texts conceals this conflict of interest, presenting the will of the elites as objective laws of physics or economics that are pointless to debate2.

Systemic Omissions and Blind Spots
Within each cluster, blind spots are carefully curated to maintain the legitimacy of the current institutional structure. In the health sector, the role of food corporations in engineering an obesogenic environment and the astronomical profits of pharma companies from engineering lifelong dependence are ignored. In the tech sphere, the identical algorithmic practices of Western platforms (Meta, Google) and their data harvesting for Western intelligence are omitted, hiding the political motive of censorship. In politics, a nation's democratic right to self-determination and the potential for debt restructuring are erased in favor of bond market math. In entertainment, the total exploitation of human labor and societal cultural degradation are swept under the rug of billion-dollar valuations.

ACTIONABLE VERDICT

FORECASTS • STRATEGY
07

ACTIONABLE VERDICT: THE ANALYTICAL RESIDUE

The fundamental, overarching narrative of the day is the total necessity of external behavioral control and the mitigation of systemic risks. States control social media algorithms and memory chip exports; pharmaceuticals seize control of metabolism and appetite; media corporations commandeer dopamine cycles via AI and micro-cinema; global debt markets dictate the borders of states. In this paradigm, the human being is framed as a source of entropy—an unpredictable glitch that must be algorithmized, pharmacologically stabilized, and financially restrained.

A reader consuming this array will likely miss the total substitution of free choice with unalterable necessity and the radical narrowing of their political imagination. Consumers of information will believe they are freely and rationally choosing to watch a film in IMAX, purchase weight-loss injections, vote against provincial succession, and support tech sanctions, utterly oblivious to the fact that the architecture of these choices was artificially engineered by institutions to maximize profit and minimize risk.

The collective texts relentlessly shift the Overton window toward the absolute normalization of biopolitical and digital paternalism. The deployment of potent chemicals into healthy adolescent bodies to adapt them to algorithmically imposed beauty standards, and the state's unconditional right to dictate the machine code of recommendation feeds, cease to be subjects of ethical debate. They become the baseline, routine conditions of the new institutional reality.

The Verdict: The institutionalization of mass total dependency through the convergence of algorithmic, biomedical, and financial governance of systemic elite risks.
