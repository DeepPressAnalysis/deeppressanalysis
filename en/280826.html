THE COGNITIVE DOMAIN AS A SPACE FOR SOCIAL PROGRAMMING

In the modern institutional paradigm, classical concepts of kinetic warfare and territorial control have given way to doctrines focused on perception management. The transformation of the security architecture, spearheaded by NATO structures, the European Union, and a pool of leading think tanks, demonstrates a fundamental shift: the human brain is now officially conceptualized as the sixth theater of war.

Under this approach, cognitive warfare is no longer viewed solely as an instrument of foreign policy suppression or an attribute of interstate conflict. It has evolved into an internal, permanent mechanism of social engineering aimed at the artificial construction of consensus, the preemptive neutralization of dissent, and the legitimization of radical macroeconomic decisions that would normally provoke public rejection.

This report provides a comprehensive structural analysis of the mechanisms through which supranational bureaucracies, the military-industrial complex, and transnational institutions execute semantic hacking, deliberately shift the Overton window, and deploy a multi-tiered infrastructure of legitimation. The ultimate goal of these coordinated information operations is the redistribution of colossal volumes of public capital. The manufactured facade of "defending democratic institutions" and "information integrity" serves as an operational cover for an unprecedented shift of the costs of forced militarization onto civil society, all to maximize the profits of an extremely narrow group of beneficiaries among military-industrial prime contractors. Perception of reality is treated by institutions not as an objective given, but as a "controlled hallucination" and a probabilistic mechanism subject to strict algorithmic calibration.

SEMANTIC HACKING: FROM "DISINFORMATION" TO FIMI

Legitimizing the radical restructuring of the economy and the extraction of social resources requires a preliminary alteration of the language society uses to comprehend these processes. Semantic hacking consists of substituting traditional, intuitively understood definitions with new, institutionally approved constructs designed to block critical thinking, trigger predefined emotional responses, and insulate government actions from democratic oversight.

The most massive example of this is the coordinated abandonment of the classical term "disinformation" by the European External Action Service (EEAS) and affiliated think tanks in favor of the FIMI (Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference) concept. The traditional anti-disinformation paradigm relied on fact-checking—verifying the truth or falsehood of specific claims. However, this approach proved structurally vulnerable for state institutions: if undesirable, anti-elitist, or anti-militarist information turned out to be factually true, blocking it was inevitably classified by the public as political censorship.

The introduction of the FIMI concept elegantly resolves this epistemological problem. According to official EEAS definitions, FIMI focuses not on the content itself (which can be absolutely accurate and based on verifiable facts), but on so-called "behavioral patterns." If objectively true information is disseminated in a coordinated manner, uses emotional triggers, breaks out of a prescribed institutional context, or is utilized to amplify political polarization at a critical moment, it is automatically classified as hostile manipulation and interference.

This radically shifts the Overton window: the existential threat is no longer falsehood per se, but any unauthorized coordination of meanings capable of undermining trust in dominant institutions. The third EEAS report officially introduced the "FIMI Exposure Matrix." This structural four-step classification tool ensures that hidden networks are attributed based solely on behavioral traits and tactics (TTPs), even if their narratives formally violate no laws. Such criminalization of behavior creates a universal mechanism for suppressing any organized civic opposition by labeling it a product of "foreign interference" or "useful idiots" acting in the enemy's interests.

"COGNITIVE SECURITY" AND EMOTIONAL ANCHORING

The second vector of semantic hacking is the active implementation of the "Cognitive Security" concept. Synchronized publications by the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS), the RAND Corporation, and the Brookings Institution illustrate a coordinated campaign to revise fundamental educational priorities. In the analytical materials of these think tanks, it is explicitly postulated that the traditional state focus on science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) was a flawed strategy, as pure scientific and technical superiority fails to provide defense against "Hostile Social Manipulation."

Think tanks are deliberately injecting the narrative that "civic education" must become an absolute national imperative to ensure cognitive security. Upon detailed analysis, it becomes evident that in this institutional context, civic education does not mean studying constitutional rights or democratic oversight mechanisms, but rather the incubation of ideological loyalty and the formation of so-called "resilience" against narratives contradicting the official line. State and defense structures are urged to heavily fund grassroots initiatives to teach citizens to "correctly" filter the information ecosystem and reject alternative interpretations of reality. This is an attempt at preemptive perception programming at the level of foundational educational institutions.

To firmly embed these new meanings within global and regional bureaucracies, the mechanism of artificial consensus generation is utilized via the World Economic Forum (WEF). In the 2025 and 2026 Global Risks Reports, disinformation and misinformation are deliberately and categorically elevated to the number one short-term global risk, surpassing even state-level armed conflicts, economic crises, and extreme weather events.

Relying on surveys of hundreds of selected experts (where 64% forecast a fragmentation of the world order), the WEF creates a powerful emotional anchor: disinformation (especially that generated by artificial intelligence algorithms) is directly linked to geoeconomic confrontation, radical societal polarization, and the potential collapse of state institutions. By projecting an apocalyptic forecast, wherein 62% of experts predict a "stormy" and turbulent trajectory over the next decade, the WEF cultivates the psychological readiness of political elites and governments to implement unprecedentedly harsh regulatory and repressive measures in the information sphere. This fear is converted into a mandate to expand the powers of the executive branch.

THE INFRASTRUCTURE OF LEGITIMATION: NATO ACT AND THE DSA

The manufacturing of public consent requires a flawless institutional assembly line: from theoretical and pseudoscientific development in closed structures to the legalization of concepts via international legal acts.

NATO ACT: The Neurocentric Approach
Within the military bloc, the systemic conceptualization of the cognitive environment is handled by NATO's Allied Command Transformation (ACT). This structure is responsible for developing the comprehensive "Cognitive Warfare Concept" as part of realizing the fundamental imperative of "Cognitive Superiority." Incubation of meanings and the development of tactical solutions occur at specialized military-science symposia (such as STO-MP-HFM-361), where cognitive warfare is analyzed in detail through a synthesis of neurobiology, social psychology, game theory, and AI systems.

Papers presented at these closed research venues (e.g., "Cognitive Warfare: Problem for the Brain, Opportunity for the Machine") formulate a paradigmatic conclusion: the biologically limited human brain physically cannot cope with the volume and velocity of data in the modern information environment. This leads to a critical deceleration in decision-making, described via John Boyd's OODA loop (Observe-Orient-Decide-Act). Consequently, AI and machine learning algorithms are positioned by NATO strategists not merely as auxiliary tools, but as absolute necessities for the machine filtration of reality and preemptive defense against cognitive threats. In ACT conceptual documents, human perception is cynically described as a "competitive construction process," where conscious experience is merely a "controlled hallucination." The institutional overarching goal of defense departments is to monopolize the right to shape this controlled hallucination for the populations of member states, cutting off competing narratives before they reach the recipient's conscious awareness.

Digital Services Act (DSA): Privatized Censorship
Theoretical frameworks, beta-tested at RAND, CSIS, and NATO, require a practical mechanism for implementation. They are legalized through the bureaucratic apparatus of the European Commission. The Digital Services Act (DSA) represents the pinnacle of the infrastructure for privatized censorship and social control. Articles 34 and 35 of the DSA mandate Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs) and Very Large Online Search Engines (VLOSEs) to conduct obligatory assessments and mitigation of so-called "systemic risks."

The key institutional trick here is the legal merging of the FIMI concept, disinformation, and the vague notion of "systemic risks" to democracy and elections. The European Commission (specifically, the DG CONNECT Directorate-General) has elegantly outsourced direct responsibility for internet censorship to private transnational corporations. Platforms are legally obligated to filter, shadowban, label, or remove content that the bureaucracy might deem as facilitating electoral manipulation or undermining civic discourse.

Thus, digital environment management platforms are forced to transition from a policy of technical neutrality to geopolitical and ideological partisanship. The threat of astronomical fines (up to 6% of global turnover) for failing to meet risk mitigation requirements forces corporations to over-insure themselves and algorithmically suppress any narratives challenging the official consensus. This is presented to society behind a humanistic facade of "creating a safe digital environment," "protecting children," and combating cyberbullying, concealing the mechanism's true nature as an instrument of structural ontological dictate.

FACADE AND REALITY: THE ECONOMICS OF STRUCTURAL MILITARIZATION

The entire array of cognitive operations, the stoking of anxiety surrounding the concepts of "defending democracy," the implementation of anti-FIMI infrastructure, and the pedaling of existential risks in Davos serve merely as a complex political and socio-engineering cover for a massive financial operation. The true objective of this multi-tiered campaign is the legitimation of the extraction of colossal public funds and their directive reallocation to the European Defence Technological and Industrial Base (EDTIB).

Readiness 2030 (ReArm Europe) and Risk Nationalization
In 2024–2025, the European Commission, under the leadership of Ursula von der Leyen, launched an unprecedented process of militarizing the European economy, the centerpiece of which was the "ReArm Europe" plan (subsequently rebranded as Readiness 2030 to avoid overly aggressive connotations). The stated institutional goal of the plan is the mobilization of an astronomical €800 billion (accounting for approximately 4.7% of total EU GDP) by the end of the decade for an accelerated build-up of defense capacities.

To execute this plan immediately, bypassing lengthy national budget approval procedures, the SAFE mechanism (Security Action for Europe — Regulation (EU) 2025/1106) was introduced. This financial instrument authorizes the European Commission to centrally borrow up to €150 billion on capital markets, which is then transferred to member states as long-term loans for the emergency procurement of priority armaments (artillery systems, missiles, air defense complexes, strike drones).

From a PR perspective, this step is sold to the electorate as an act of European solidarity and necessary defense. However, the cynical structural reality of the SAFE mechanism is a sharp surge in the sovereign debt burden of member states. Citizens (taxpayers) absorb all the financial risks of these loans to guarantee payment for long-term contracts awarded to defense conglomerates. To facilitate these payments, governments are officially permitted to ignore the strict fiscal rules of the Stability and Growth Pact, allowing national budget deficits to exceed the established 3% threshold and driving up state debt. Costs are nationalized; profits are cartelized.

REPROGRAMMING COHESION FUNDS AND SEMANTIC PROTECTIONISM

Debt financing via SAFE is supplemented by the direct and aggressive redistribution of existing EU budget funds. The published European Defence Industrial Strategy (EDIS) and its accompanying European Defence Industry Programme (EDIP, Regulation (EU) 2025/2643) forge the new regulatory framework for this process. EDIP, with its initial target budget of €1.5 billion for the 2025-2027 period, is merely a legal catalyst; its primary function lies not in the volume of these specific funds, but in the introduction of new legal structures: SEAP (Structure for European Armament Programme) and EDPCI (European Defence Project of Common Interest).

The most revealing aspect of this capital transfer operation is the reprogramming of Cohesion Funds, primarily the European Regional Development Fund (ERDF). Historically, these multi-billion-dollar funds were established to equalize living standards across various European regions, invest in social infrastructure, develop depressed territories, combat the mounting housing crisis, and support vulnerable populations.

However, during the mid-term budget review, the European Commission leveraged the political lever of an "emergency situation" to grant member states the legal right to redirect these social resources into "productive investments" for defense sector enterprises. Unprecedentedly, the Commission abolished geographical and enterprise-size restrictions, allowing money intended for social needs to be funneled to major military conglomerates to modernize explosive and propellant manufacturing plants and subsidize dual-use military technologies. The institutional narrative cynically justifies this slashing of social guarantees (such as affordable housing and healthcare) as necessitated by "industrial readiness" and security demands.

Semantic Protectionism and the "Buy European" Doctrine
The operation to unprecedentedly enrich European prime contractors requires the meticulous elimination of competition, including from close allies like the US and UK. The SEAP and EDPCI instruments, legalized under EDIP, allow nations to enter into cooperative agreements with guaranteed VAT exemptions and legal bypasses of the strict public procurement transparency rules established by Directive 2009/81/EC.

Effectively, Article 346 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), which historically allowed countries to ignore single competitive market rules exclusively in the extreme interests of "essential national security," is now routinely utilized to monopolize contracts for a narrow circle of privileged suppliers. The SAFE and EDIP regulations firmly enshrine the protectionist "Buy European" principle at the normative level. A strict rule has been established: the value of components originating from third countries (non-EU or EFTA) can under no circumstances exceed 35% of the estimated value of the final product's components. Furthermore, full design authority and executive control of contracting companies must belong exclusively to European entities.

This deliberate restriction causes severe structural friction with the United States. Washington exerts colossal diplomatic pressure on the EU, demanding American corporate access to expanding European defense budgets (e.g., aggressively lobbying for the procurement of fifth-generation F-35 fighters over European programs like Eurofighter Tranche-5 or the prospective FCAS). The European bureaucracy, hiding behind the narrative of achieving "strategic autonomy," attempts to build a protectionist wall around its internal market. This leads to a schizophrenic rift: critical dependence on the US for high-tech and strategic platforms (like the F-35) remains, while the European military-industrial complex leverages administrative resources to consolidate and capture the production of basic systems, munitions, and electronics.

ARTIFICIAL DEFICITS AND THE CONSOLIDATION OF SUPER-PROFITS

The results of this massive institutional engineering are glaringly apparent in financial metrics. Coordinated media campaigns, stoking fear of an imminent external threat and pervasive FIMI cognitive attacks, have ensured that the European defense market (according to forecasts) will grow from $448 billion in 2025 to $724 billion by 2032, demonstrating an anomalous compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of over 7%.

It is crucial to note that costs in this sector are artificially inflated. A deep audit reveals that the cost of a basic 155mm artillery shell (NATO standard) surged from €950 (during the 2019-2021 period) to an astonishing €5,500 under NSPA contracts by 2024-2025. Concurrently, a detailed bottom-up cost accounting analysis of supply chains proves that the actual rise in objective costs for raw materials (such as tungsten, copper, RDX precursors) and overtime labor premiums accounts for no more than 30% of this price hike. The remaining 60-70% of the inflated price is pure margin (speculative inflation), settling directly into the accounts of Tier-1 companies. The deficit is being consciously monetized.

European prime contractors, such as BAE Systems, Thales, Rheinmetall, and Leonardo, are demonstrating record-breaking, historically unprecedented financial performance. BAE Systems officially reported a record order backlog of £83.6 billion and a 12% increase in underlying operating profit. Thales and Leonardo stand as the primary institutional recipients of grants from the European Defence Fund (EDF) and new programs creating the "cognitive shield," capturing the lion's share of multi-million-euro subsidies for research, military cloud systems, and cyber defense architecture.

INSTITUTIONAL ASSESSMENT: THE ARCHITECTURE OF EXTRACTION

Structural analysis, stripped of its ideological veneer, convincingly proves that so-called "cognitive security," the fight against "disinformation," and new regulatory frameworks (FIMI) represent nothing more than a complex administrative and socio-engineering facade. The declarations of Western think tanks regarding the vital necessity of countering "hostile social manipulation" and initiatives for unprecedentedly strict algorithmic regulation of digital platforms via the DSA mechanism serve one pragmatic, institutional objective: the preemptive suppression of organized civic dissent amid a radical shift in state macroeconomic policy.

Before our eyes, a classic scheme, refined to structural perfection, is unfolding: the offloading of massive excess costs onto civil society to maximize and guarantee the profits of financial-industrial elites. Through the debt mechanisms of the SAFE instrument, sovereign states are committing to tens of billions of euros in loans with capitalizing interest, while through the legal reprogramming of Cohesion Funds, basic social investments are cynically and legally converted into direct subsidies for the military industry.

Restrictive, protectionist clauses regarding joint procurement (the SEAP and EDPCI institutions), coupled with the exploitation of TFEU Article 346, legally guarantee that the colossal pools of fiat liquidity generated in this manner will be monopolized and absorbed by the European defense cartel. In this architecture, cognitive warfare is waged by supranational institutions not so much against hypothetical foreign actors, but against their own taxpayers. Consent for the dismantling of the historical welfare state is continuously generated, strictly moderated, and forcefully maintained at both the semantic and algorithmic levels. The infrastructure of the cognitive domain ensures the silence of this expropriation.
