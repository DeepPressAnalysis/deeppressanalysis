U.S. Treasury Proposed Rule (31 CFR Part 851) — Prohibition on Outbound Investments in Super Intelligence (SI) Systems, HBM Memory (>2 GB/s/mm²), Photonics, and Quantum Networks in the PRC and Russian Federation. On October 26, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Investment Security published a proposed rule for the Outbound Investment Security Program (31 CFR Part 851, Docket No. TREAS-DO-2026-0595, RIN 1505-AC94, Document No. 2026-21786), with a public comment period open until December 7, 2026. The proposed regulation replaces and substantially expands the prior Part 850 rules for U.S. persons and their controlled foreign entities in transactions involving persons from "Countries of Concern" under § 851.207: the PRC (including Hong Kong and Macau), the Russian Federation, Iran, the DPRK, Cuba, and the former regime of N. Maduro in Venezuela (noting that on March 5, 2026, the United States recognized the government of Delcy Rodriguez).

Key technical parameters of the regulation encompass four blocks. First, regarding AI systems and Super Intelligence (SI), the new Part 851 completely eliminates training compute power thresholds measured in FLOPs, upon which the prior Part 850 relied. The Treasury noted that modern models achieve performance gains through synthetic data, algorithmic efficiency, post-training, and inference-time compute; consequently, the development of any Super Intelligence (SI) system is classified as Prohibited Technology without any notification threshold. Second, in the semiconductor and microelectronics sector, a direct prohibition is introduced on investments in the development or production of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) with a bandwidth density exceeding 2 gigabytes per second per square millimeter (>2 GB/s/mm², synchronized with ECCN 3A090), any electronic design automation (EDA) software for integrated circuit design or advanced packaging, front-end equipment for volume chip production (including EUV and DUV lithography) and advanced packaging, as well as photonic integrated circuits (including optical components, co-packaged optics CPO, and silicon photonics), specialized DRAM memory, radiation-hardened ICs, and Group III-V / ultra-wide bandgap semiconductors. Third, in high-performance computing (HPC), transactions involving supercomputers with a capacity of $\ge 100$ petaflops of double-precision (64-bit) or $\ge 200$ petaflops of single-precision (32-bit) processing within a volume of $\le 41,600$ cubic feet are prohibited, whereas the development of high-speed, low-latency interconnects (for SoC, SiP, scale-up, and scale-out networks) not falling under the prohibition requires mandatory notification as Notifiable Technology. Fourth, all quantum technologies (quantum sensors for military/intelligence applications and quantum networks/communications, including quantum computer scaling and QKD) are moved into the complete prohibition category, and the sectors of hypersonic systems and biotechnology are added to the scope of the program.

The document closes channels through which U.S. venture, corporate, and private capital, as well as foreign subsidiaries of U.S. corporations, could participate in joint ventures, licensing, or financing of Chinese developers of AI models, silicon photonics (CPO), HBM memory, and quantum communications. Abandoning the FLOPs metric means that any investment ties with Chinese AI laboratories in advanced modeling shift from the compliance-notification zone into the direct prohibition zone. Annual compliance burdens are estimated by the Treasury at 18,000 hours and at least $2.31 million across ~150 reporting entities per year. While this increases regulatory fragmentation and compliance costs for semiconductor, optical, and EDA companies with an Asian presence—including Nvidia (NVDA), AMD, Intel (INTC), Micron Technology (MU), Broadcom (AVGO), Synopsys (SNPS), Cadence Design Systems (CDNS), Applied Materials (AMAT), Lam Research (LRCX), KLA Corp (KLAC), ASML, and hyperscalers (MSFT, GOOGL, META, AMZN)—over the medium term it protects the technological market share of Western HBM manufacturers such as Micron and AI infrastructure providers outside the PRC.

Counter-argument: Because 31 CFR Part 851 is currently a Proposed Rule open for public comment through December 7, 2026, its near-term operational impact is not yet legally binding, and industry feedback regarding the broad scope of "Super Intelligence" and interconnect thresholds could result in refined technical definitions before a final rule is promulgated.

BIS Extension of the Suspension of the "50% Rule" for Subsidiaries of Entity List Parties Until January 10, 2027. On November 9, 2026 (91 FR 70789, Document No. 2026-22793, effective November 9, 2026), the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) of the U.S. Department of Commerce issued a final rule extending the stay of its Interim Final Rule (IFR) titled "Expansion of End-User Controls to Cover Affiliates of Certain Listed Entities" (published September 30, 2025 at 90 FR 47201 and initially suspended for one year on November 12, 2025 at 90 FR 50857). Under the original rule, any foreign entity owned 50% or more, directly or indirectly, by one or more parties on the Entity List (or certain other restricted lists) automatically becomes subject to the same EAR licensing restrictions as the parent company. The prior one-year suspension was set to expire on November 9, 2026; under the new BIS action, the rule's effect is once again suspended—but only for two months, until January 10, 2027. BIS noted that previously submitted public comments were not considered for this technical extension, but will be analyzed for subsequent regulatory actions. For global suppliers of semiconductor equipment, industrial automation, EDA software, and electronic components (AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, ASML, NVDA, AMD, INTC, QCOM, TXN, SNPS, CDNS), November 9, 2026 was a critical threshold date after which unlisted subsidiaries and joint ventures of Entity List parties would have lost the ability to receive U.S. technology without BIS licenses. The two-month reprieve until January 10, 2027 preserves the status quo and allows exporters to complete Q4 2026 calendar shipments without disruption; however, the short 62-day extension increment instead of a full year suggests a high likelihood that restrictions, or a revised version thereof, will be implemented after January 10, 2027.

Counter-argument: While the 62-day extension of the stay points toward potential implementation of affiliate restrictions in January 2027, BIS explicitly stated that previously submitted public comments have not yet been incorporated into a final determination, leaving open the possibility of further procedural modifications or targeted exemptions once those comments are formally reviewed.

Final Market Synthesis. The convergence of signals across the five examined perimeters points toward a structural regrouping of capital into a "second phase" of the AI, semiconductor, and national security cycle. Regarding the direction of capital flows, major hedge funds (Lone Pine, D1 Capital, Duquesne, Appaloosa) have been locking in profits in the most visible first-wave winners—independent power generators (VST, TLN) and select chip designers (AVGO, SNDK, partially NVDA)—and redirecting resources toward three segments: (1) physical and cloud bottlenecks in AI infrastructure trading at lower valuation bases—specialized neoclouds (NBIS, CRWV), server systems (HPE), industrial data center cooling (JCI), storage and memory systems (STX, MU), and the restructuring business of Intel (INTC), where Coatue's entry ($1.69 billion) and CEO Lip-Bu Tan's personal purchase ($9.71 million) coincided; (2) defensive financial monopolies and Treasury liquidity, reflected in Berkshire Hathaway's reserve ($356.9–$377.4 billion) alongside the simultaneous resumption of buybacks ($9.6 billion in Q3) and net equity purchases (+$11.2 billion YTD), as well as Pershing Square's $3.27 billion entry into V, MA, and SPGI; and (3) biotechnology and molecular diagnostics, where specialized insiders (Baker Bros., OrbiMed, Arch Venture, RA Capital) deployed over $260 million across two weeks alongside Druckenmiller's retention of a 16.6% portfolio weight in NTRA.

Government and budgetary support is concentrating in software-defined command and control (C2) systems, military cyber operations, and layered missile and counter-UAV defense (IAMD/C-UAS). The deployment of the U.S. Army's NGC2 program with a ceiling of up to $1.8 billion (Anduril / PLTR + $93.6 million for GD and 8 software contractors), LMT's victory in the Future Interceptor PAC-3 Edge competition ($1.08 billion), LDOS's contract package with USCYBERCOM and TSA ($931 million), and the European Commission and Frontex allocation of €300 million for drones and counter-drone systems provide direct revenue visibility for 2027–2031.

The most stringent regulatory pressure is concentrated in cross-border semiconductor supply chains, EDA software, and quantum-optical computing: the U.S. Treasury's proposed rule 31 CFR Part 851 (comments open until December 7, 2026) and the approaching expiration of the BIS "50% rule" stay (January 10, 2027) establish barriers to capital and technological links with China in the segments of Super Intelligence (SI), HBM memory (>2 GB/s/mm²), silicon photonics, and lithography. Additionally, data center operators in Texas (GLXY, CRWV) faced a short-term political-regulatory repricing of power interconnection timelines following the midterm elections.

Over a 30-to-90-day horizon, potential beneficiaries include LDOS (converting $1.25 billion in USCYBERCOM, TSA, and DHA contracts into backlog), PLTR, LMT, and LHX (key recipients of NGC2, TITAN, PAC-3 Edge, and C-UAS budgets), KMI (protected natural gas pipeline cash flows and a $15 million share purchase by its chairman), MDGL and NTRA (backing from specialized funds and transitions to profitability/positive cash flow), as well as GLXY and INTC (stabilization potential following CEO purchases of nearly $10 million in each stock).
