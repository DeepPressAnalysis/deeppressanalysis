MEDIA FRAMES AND SYSTEMIC OMISSIONS

The global information picture of September 9, 2026, is constructed using three dominant media frames, each serving as an instrument to legitimize specific elite interests.

  • The "Institutionalization of Innovation" Frame (Market Inevitability): Actively used by financial publications (especially WSJ) to describe processes surrounding the AI industry. Under this frame, the unprofitability of OpenAI and Anthropic is presented not as a fundamental risk, but as a temporary phase of aggressive growth.17 This frame securely conceals the essence of the process—an unprecedented operation to externalize risks (shifting Big Tech's liabilities onto pension funds).
  • The "Defense of Sovereignty" Frame (National Interest): Dominates the domestic political and foreign trade discourse in the US and Canada. Actions by Mark Carney to impose 50% tariffs26 and Donald Trump's rhetoric supporting data centers4 are wrapped in the guise of protecting the national economy. This hides the fact that ordinary citizens will inevitably pay for these initiatives through inflation, rising electricity tariffs, and increased national debt.
  • The "Crisis of the World Order" Frame (Existential Threat): Most prominently featured in the European press (FT) and statements by international organizations (CSG) regarding the situation in global shipping.2 The growth of the shadow fleet is described using terms like "fragmentation," "collapse," and "ecological disaster."21 This intentionally masks reality: the "shadow" logistical system has proven to be a highly adaptive and economically efficient alternative for the Global South to trade outside the monopoly of Western insurance cartels.

The analysis also uncovers a layer of critically important information that is systematically excluded from global media circulation. First, there are the physical and energy limits of the AI industry. Despite political battles over data centers in the US,11 the media avoids large-scale analysis of the base electricity generation deficit. It is silenced that the scaling plans declared by OpenAI and Anthropic are physically unfeasible within the current power grid configuration, rendering their multi-trillion dollar valuations speculative fiction.33

Second, there is a strict taboo on discussing the ultimate payers of the Hormuz crisis. The press extensively covers drone strikes,3 but does not publish calculations on how a $4.5 million increase in insurance for a single tanker40 translates into specific fractions of a percent of European and Asian inflation. The mechanism of extracting funds from consumers' pockets in favor of a narrow group of reinsurance corporations is completely ignored.12
