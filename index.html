RESEARCH RESULT

MACRO STRATEGY • TECTONIC SHIFT
01

THE MAIN RESEARCH RESULT

The drop of aggregated natural gas reserves in the underground gas storage facilities of the European Union (EU-27 UGS) below the critical threshold of 10% of technical active gas volume (less than 113.27 TWh, or ~10.32 billion cubic meters out of a total capacity of 1,132.70 TWh / ~103.16 bcm) by March 31, 2027, has transitioned from the realm of theoretical stress tests to practical energy risk management. As of November 5, 2026, the European gas system entered the 2026/2027 heating season with the lowest storage fill level since 2013—74.62% (845.221 TWh for the gas day of October 31 / November 1, 2026, according to GIE AGSI+ data), failing to meet even the European Commission's reduced target mandate of 80%. A report published by the International Energy Agency (IEA Global Gas Security Review 2026) on November 3, 2026, explicitly stated that according to IEA simulations, the combination of colder-than-average winter weather and the ongoing blockade of LNG supplies through the Strait of Hormuz could cause EU UGS reserves to fall below 10% of active capacity by the end of March 2027.

Multivariate stochastic modeling (250,000 Monte Carlo realizations, 65,536 Sobol quasi-random sequences, and 100,000 Latin hypercube samples), calibrated against October and November balances from ENTSOG, ACER, and the IEA, demonstrates that the unconditional model probability of EU-27 aggregated reserves falling below 10.0% by March 31, 2027, stands at 12.22% (an expert-model range accounting for parametric uncertainty of 11.5%–14.0%). The probability of completely exhausting all commercially available reserves and hitting the untouchable threshold of national strategic reserves (11.0% of capacity, or 124.60 TWh) reaches 17.06%. Furthermore, due to sharp spatial fragmentation of reserves within the EU, the probability of storage falling below 10% in the Northwestern and Central European industrial core (Germany, the Netherlands, Austria)—which started on November 1, 2026, with a fill level of only 63.46% (including Germany at 61.03%–61.5%, or ~151.1 TWh)—is 61.28%, while in Germany itself it is 67.15%.

If the studied state ("EU-27 UGS below 10% by the end of March 2027") becomes a reality, a non-linear cascade of consequences across three orders is projected to occur: ranging from a physical drop in daily withdrawal capacity from UGS by 60%–75% due to the loss of reservoir pressure and the forced rationing of unprotected industrial consumers (Emergency level under EU Regulation 2017/1938) in February–March 2027, to the structural impossibility of replenishing storage in the summer of 2027. Exiting the winter with a residual of 8.5%–10% would require a net summer injection of 810–923 TWh (73.8–84.1 bcm) to reach the 80%–90% targets by November 1, 2027—which is 65%–88% higher than the actual injection of the crisis summer of 2026 (490 TWh / 43 bcm) and is capable of prolonging the global LNG deficit and high TTF prices throughout 2027 and 2028.

SYSTEM STATE

ENERGY SECURITY • LIQUIDITY
02

CURRENT SYSTEM STATE

The reconstruction of the actual state of the European and global gas system based on primary documents from GIE AGSI+, ENTSOG, ACER, and the IEA for the first week of November 2026 reveals a profound structural imbalance accumulated over the previous eight months.

The critical vulnerability of the European gas balance by November 2026 was formed as a result of the convergence of five fundamental processes developing since early 2026. The first link was the depletion of the buffer during the cold winter of 2025/2026: withdrawal from UGS began late (in mid-November 2025) but sharply accelerated in the first quarter of 2026 due to lower temperatures and an increase in gas consumption in power generation by 34 TWh (plus 18 TWh of growth in residential and industrial sectors). Over the 2025/2026 winter, about 620 TWh (~56 bcm) was withdrawn from EU storage. By early April 2026, the aggregated EU UGS level had dropped to 27%–28% (314 TWh / ~29 bcm), compared to 34% a year earlier and a 40% average over 9 years. In the Netherlands, reserves plummeted to a 10-year low of 5.8% by March 25, 2026, with the Alkmaar and Grijpskerk storage facilities filled to less than 1%.

The second, defining link was the largest LNG supply shock in history at the Strait of Hormuz (beginning February 28, 2026). In late February 2026, a military conflict flared up in the Middle East involving the US, Israel, and Iran. In March 2026, airstrikes on gas processing plants in the Assaluyeh industrial complex disabled about 12% of Iran's gas production, and damaged two LNG liquefaction trains in the region (17% of pre-war capacity), which will remain offline for several years. The transit of loaded gas carriers through the Strait of Hormuz completely ceased in March 2026 and averaged only 10 ships per month from April to September 2026, compared to 95 ships per month in 2025 (with a partial recovery to 27 ships in September). As a result, LNG shipments from Qatar and the UAE plummeted by over 60 bcm (-63% y/y) between March and October 2026. For the EU, this meant the loss of 95% of Qatari LNG imports from April 2026.

The third process was the global LNG deficit and the price shock of 2026. Although projects outside the Persian Gulf increased LNG production by 14% (over 40 bcm y/y for March–October), they could only compensate for two-thirds of the Middle Eastern losses. Based on the IEA forecast, global LNG supply will show a record historical drop of 3% for all of 2026. Competition for available cargoes led to an almost 80% y/y increase in spot prices in Asia (averaging $21.5/MMBtu) and a 70%–90% y/y increase in TTF prices in Europe. In October 2026, amid unscheduled maintenance in Norway and Houthi threats against Saudi Arabian facilities, TTF quotes broke the 100 EUR/MWh mark for the first time since 2022, reaching 103.94–104.90 EUR/MWh.

The fourth factor was the inversion of seasonal spreads and the failure of commercial UGS auctions (the "Rehden Case"). Because the market priced the current 2026 LNG deficit higher than the risks of 2027, the seasonal spread between winter and summer contracts on TTF (Winter-Summer spread) remained negative throughout the summer of 2026 (backwardation averaging -1.60 EUR/MWh). Commercial gas injection into UGS without government subsidies locked in a direct loss for traders. Consequently, net injection into EU UGS over the summer of 2026 was only 43 bcm (~490 TWh), compared to a 10-year average of 47 bcm (~540 TWh). On the PRISMA platform, firm capacities for Germany's largest UGS, Rehden, were auctioned three times with 0 GWh of bids received.

The fifth factor involved the weakening of EU regulatory mandates and delayed state interventions. Fearing that mandatory gas purchasing at any price with a closed Strait of Hormuz would drive TTF even higher, the European Commission in 2026 reduced the pan-European storage fill target for November 1 from 90% to 80%. The Netherlands officially lowered its national target from 74% to 64%, while the German government mandated the state-owned company SEFE to purchase an additional 8 TWh of gas by mid-December.

CAUSAL CHAINS AND SIMULATIONS

RISK MANAGEMENT • MODELING
03

CAUSAL CHAINS AND SIMULATIONS

To reconstruct all physically and economically consistent pathways from the starting point of November 1, 2026 (845.221 TWh, or 74.62%) to the final state on March 31, 2027 (less than 113.27 TWh, or less than 10.0%), a reverse causal modeling approach was applied (Fault Tree Analysis). Reaching a level below 10% for the EU-27 cannot occur due to a single isolated factor under all other equal conditions, as dropping reserves below 25%–20% triggers powerful negative feedback loops: a spike in TTF prices above 130–180 EUR/MWh causes industrial demand destruction, and the drop in reservoir pressure limits the speed of withdrawal. For the system to overcome these barriers, a primary (gross) balance deficit prior to market adaptation must reach 305–340 TWh.

Reverse modeling identifies four main classes of causal pathways. The first and primary class is the IEA geopolitical-climate doublet (conditional probability of breaking 10% is 68.86%, and 97.54% under extreme cold): a continued blockade of the Strait of Hormuz combined with a cold winter and the January 1, 2027 enforcement of a total EU ban on Russian LNG imports under long-term contracts (EU Regulation 2026/261). The second class is a convergence of moderate factors without a single extreme shock (conditional probability 45.72%–48.48%): moderately cool weather, a two-week wind drought (Dunkelflaute), moderate Asian JKM index premiums, minor Norwegian disruptions, and moderate Ukrainian withdrawal. The third class is an infrastructure-transit cascade under normal weather (conditional probability 28.82%): unscheduled accidents on key Norwegian gas hubs synchronized with a halt in TurkStream supplies due to US OFAC secondary sanctions. The fourth class is the regulatory trap (the price cap paradox).

TRAJECTORY ANALYSIS AND CASCADES

SYSTEMIC CASCADES • ALTERNATIVE PATHS
04

TRAJECTORY ANALYSIS AND CASCADES

During the generation and selection of scenarios, the combination space was compressed into 16 fundamentally different causal trajectories encompassing paths leading to a breach of the 10% threshold, as well as boundary and stabilizing trajectories where the outcome is not reached. The most likely path to breaching 10% is the "IEA Doublet" (Trajectory T-01). The initial conditions for this trajectory include the depressed transit of LNG through the Strait of Hormuz and a cold January-February 2027. Following the January 1, 2027 ban on Russian LNG, European buyers attempting to replace missing volumes on the US spot market face fierce Asian competition. The EU breaches the 30% threshold in early February, physical withdrawal speeds decline, and by late March, the 11% commercial threshold is breached, ending the winter at an average 8.32%.

Another profound path is Trajectory T-10: "Solidarity Paralysis and Regional Collapse of Germany." The geographical distribution of reserves in the EU by November 2026 is unprecedentedly skewed. Italy and France are fully secured, while Germany entered the season with significantly less gas than the previous year. When Germany's reserves drop below 10% in early 2027, an acute institutional conflict surrounding the solidarity mechanism will arise. Article 13 of EU Regulation 2017/1938 defines solidarity as a measure of last resort; consequently, Italy and France are not obligated to deplete their strategic reserves to supply German chemical or steel conglomerates. Germany will plunge below 5%–8% with severe industrial rationing, while the EU-wide indicator remains at 11.5%–14.0%.

Exiting the winter on March 31, 2027, with reserves at 8.50% will destroy the seasonal cycle of the European gas market for the entirety of 2027, making it physically and commercially impossible to fulfill the 90% injection mandate by November 1, 2027. This structural crisis of summer injection requires the EU to pump 923.2 TWh (84.1 bcm) at an average speed of 4.31 TWh/day to reach 90%—an 88.4% increase over the summer of 2026. This record injection demand will coincide with the September 30, 2027, ban on Russian pipeline gas via TurkStream. Modeling indicates that with ongoing Hormuz constraints, maximum net injection in summer 2027 will only bring the EU to a 53.5%–62.8% fill level by November 1, 2027.

THRESHOLDS, INDICATORS, AND VERDICT

FORECASTS • STRATEGY
05

THRESHOLDS, INDICATORS, AND VERDICT

The analysis of non-linear linkages and stochastic simulations revealed several non-obvious systemic effects that alter the standard perception of European gas security. The "Illusionary Buffer" between 30% and 11% means the crisis hits long before zero. Because 11.0% of capacity is locked as national strategic reserves, an AGSI+ metric of 30% only leaves 19% of capacity for the commercial market. Moreover, after storage exits the pressure plateau, the limiting factor is no longer the remaining volume, but the daily extraction speed, stripping the system's ability to cover peak cold days.

Furthermore, a self-reinforcing cascade emerges through the fertilizer and food markets. The shutdown of ammonia production in Europe, typically viewed as a positive stabilizer for gas storage, will directly translate into a physical shortage of nitrogen fertilizers for the spring 2027 sowing season due to the concurrent unavailability of Middle Eastern fertilizer imports. The Middle East conflict has damaged global fertilizer supply chains, making imported alternatives unaffordably scarce for European farmers.
