SOVEREIGN SHIFT

MACRO STRATEGY • TECTONIC SHIFT
01

THE MAIN TECTONIC SHIFT

In response to the structural collapse of institutional demand for long-term debt and the uncontrolled expansion of fiscal deficits (expressed by the breakout of US 30-year Treasury bonds past the 5.3% level), states are transitioning to direct financial repression and hidden yield curve management by Ministries of Finance. This fundamentally alters the market architecture, turning central banks into agents of fiscal dominance, and structural inflation into the sole alternative mechanism for extracting wealth and liquidating sovereign leverage.

DEBT & LIQUIDITY ARCHITECTURE

FISCAL DOMINANCE • LIQUIDITY
02

ARCHITECTURE OF DEBT & LIQUIDITY

The global financial architecture has entered the terminal phase of open fiscal dominance. The classic paradigm, in which central banks operate independently of government fiscal needs, has been de facto destroyed. Under conditions where the accumulated sovereign debt of developed countries has exceeded the mathematical limits of organic servicing, monetary policy is completely subordinated to the imperative of saving government balance sheets. The current array of data for the second half of 2026 demonstrates a tectonic gap between the public statements of regulators regarding the fight against inflation and the real emergency mechanisms pumping markets with hidden liquidity.

The epicenter of the debt shock remains the United States, where the national debt has closely approached the $40 trillion mark ($39.94 trillion as of August 2026)1. The mathematics of this debt have acquired a self-sustaining, destructive character: in just the first 10 months of fiscal year 2026, the US budget deficit reached $1.8 trillion, adding $431 billion in the single month of July2. This level of borrowing, equivalent to nearly $6 billion a day, is an absolute historical anomaly for an economy that is formally not in a recession2. As a result, interest expenses for servicing the debt have exceeded $1 trillion a year, surpassing the US defense budget and becoming the fastest-growing line item in the federal budget3.

The capital market has reacted to this supply overhang by demanding a radically higher term premium. The yield at auctions for new 30-year US Treasury bonds reached 5.22%–5.33%, securing highs not seen since 2001–20071. The bid-to-cover ratio at the 30-year bond auction stood at 2.39, but indirect bids (an indicator of foreign demand) fell to 66.8%1. This shift indicates that global capital is refusing to absorb American debt emission under previous conditions, demanding compensation for growing inflationary and fiscal risks.

Under conditions where the Federal Reserve (Fed) formally holds the base rate at a restrictive level of 3.50%–3.75% (with a median forecast of raising it to 3.8% by the end of 2026), the US Department of the Treasury is forced to take on the functions of a shadow central bank5. Hidden liquidity and yield curve management are now realized through interventions by the American Treasury. As of September 9, 2026, the US Treasury doubled the limits of its buyback program for old, illiquid (off-the-run) Treasury bonds with maturities of 10–20 and 20–30 years from $2 billion to a level of at least $4 billion per operation4. This mechanism represents disguised quantitative easing (Stealth QE) and a direct form of financial repression10.

The mechanics of this process are extremely cynical: The Treasury finances the buyout of long-term bonds through the aggressive issuance of short-term Treasury bills (T-bills)10. The state artificially absorbs duration from the market, flattening the yield curve and lowering the cost of long-term borrowing. This directly contradicts the Fed's stated anti-inflationary policy, loosening financial conditions at a time when the central bank is attempting to tighten them11. Claims that this operation is dictated exclusively by concern for "market liquidity" mask the real motive—the administration's panic in the face of an uncontrolled rise in the cost of servicing debt at the far end of the curve4.

This US policy contrasts sharply with the suicidal trajectory of the UK, where the Bank of England acts as a global macroeconomic isolate. The British regulator, holding the rate at 3.75%, continues its policy of strict Active Quantitative Tightening (Active QT), selling government bonds from its balance sheet14. While the US Treasury is buying up debt, the Bank of England is methodically dumping long bonds (Gilts) onto the market, setting a target balance sheet reduction of £70 billion (down to a level of £488 billion) for the period from October 2025 to September 202616. In the third quarter of 2026, the regulator is holding auctions to sell £725 million of short-term bonds and £600 million of medium-term bonds16.

The consequence of this dogmatic approach has been the absolute rout of the British debt market. The refusal to manage the yield curve has led to 10-year Gilt yields exceeding 5.3%, and 30-year yields closely approaching 6%—levels unseen since the late 1990s18. The situation is exacerbated by the fact that the UK Debt Management Office (DMO) is simultaneously forced to issue new bonds totaling £246.2 billion in the 2026-27 fiscal year20. The Bank of England and the DMO are essentially competing for the same pool of drying liquidity, which analysts have explicitly dubbed a "moron premium"19. Worse still, the active sales of bonds crystallize colossal losses on the balance sheet of the Bank of England itself, which, under guarantees, are estimated at up to £125 billion and are directly passed on to the Treasury, exponentially worsening the fiscal crisis21.

In the Eurozone, the European Central Bank (ECB) demonstrates a completely different approach to managing sovereign risk. Maintaining the deposit rate at 2.25% and the marginal lending rate at 2.65%26, the ECB relies entirely on unstated anti-fragmentation mechanisms to rescue peripheral economies. This is vividly illustrated by the dynamics of spreads: the gap in yield between 10-year Italian government bonds (BTP) and German bonds (Bunds) has been artificially compressed to 84.6 basis points (BTP yield is 4.22%, Bunds — 3.37%)22. By comparison, at the peak of the debt crisis, this spread exceeded 556 basis points22. The political narrative attributes this to the "stability" of the Italian government; however, in reality, this spread is maintained exclusively by the ECB's implicit guarantee to absorb any excess sovereign periphery risk onto the system's shared balance sheet24. There is no longer a free market for sovereign debt in Europe—there is only a politically managed function masking the absence of fundamental fiscal discipline.

A tectonic shift is occurring in Japan, which for decades served as a global donor of ultra-cheap liquidity. The Bank of Japan (BOJ), which holds colossal volumes of government debt on its balance sheet (around 518–519 trillion yen)27, has historically abandoned its policy of yield curve control (YCC) and initiated a normalization cycle, raising the rate to 1.00%5. The consequences for the global liquidity architecture are catastrophic. The yield on 10-year Japanese Government Bonds (JGB) broke through the 3% level—an absolute high since 1996—while the yield on 2-year bonds, which are most sensitive to monetary policy, reached 1.83%25.

This process shatters the foundation of the global carry trade. Capital that was funded in cheap yen to purchase Western assets is being forced to repatriate. In a world where 10-year JGBs yield 3% with no currency risk for domestic investors, Japanese banks, insurers, and pension funds are beginning to massively dump American and European bonds25. The yield spread between Germany and Japan is rapidly narrowing (down to 33.4 bps), reflecting this systemic capital outflow23. This creates a structural vacuum of liquidity in the sovereign debt markets of the US and the EU, forcing their Treasuries to resort to manipulations like the American buybacks program to prevent the collapse of auctions.

In Asia, the People's Bank of China (PBOC) is maneuvering between the need to rescue its stagnating real estate sector and defending the yuan from a devaluation shock. The Chinese economy has faced a slowdown in growth rates (GDP in the second quarter of 2026 grew by only 4.3% year-over-year, lagging behind the target of 4.5%–5.0%)29. In response, the PBOC has shifted to a "moderately loose" monetary policy regime, avoiding large-scale cuts to key rates (holding them steady in August 2026) so as not to provoke capital flight amid high rates in the US31. Instead, China uses targeted instruments: lowering reserve requirement ratios (RRR), reverse repo operations, and structural credit lines, keeping the cost of social borrowing at a historically low level of around 3.1%29.

SYSTEMIC REDISTRIBUTION

RISK MANAGEMENT • WEALTH EXPROPRIATION
03

SYSTEMIC REDISTRIBUTION OF RISKS

The apparatus of the Western state has been definitively reconfigured for the emergency expropriation of wealth to preserve the functionality of the sovereign balance sheet and rescue systemically important institutions. The debt crisis of 2026 demonstrates a classic pattern: the privatization of profits by financial elites and the nationalization of losses, followed by passing the bill to the real sector.

The nationalization of losses is most starkly realized through the aforementioned interventions by the US Treasury. During the era of zero-interest-rate policies, primary dealers, major hedge funds, and institutional investors accumulated massive volumes of long-term bonds on their balance sheets. As rates rose, these assets (off-the-run Treasuries) found themselves deeply underwater, threatening the system with a cascade of margin calls and a liquidity crisis11. By doubling the volume of buybacks for these old papers to $4 billion per operation, the US Treasury is essentially bailing out Wall Street. Interest rate (duration) risk is forcibly removed from the balance sheets of private corporations and transferred back onto the state's sovereign balance sheet10. However, the state cannot destroy this risk; it only transforms it. By replacing long-term debt with short-term bills, the Treasury makes America itself extremely vulnerable to refinancing shocks10. A similar picture is observed in the UK, where taxpayers are directly covering the £125 billion in losses incurred by the Bank of England from the sell-off of QE-era assets21. The state is rescuing financial markets at the cost of its own fiscal sovereignty.

The privatization of profits in this cycle is concentrated in the banking sector and among corporations with high debt loads. The suppression of yields at the far end of the curve (expressed in the immediate pullback of 30-year US bond yields from 5.33% to below 5.2% following the announcement of the buyback program4) lowers the cost of corporate lending. Large businesses and private equity funds gain the ability to refinance their obligations at rates artificially depressed by state intervention11. Concurrently, global infrastructure projects tied to the expansion of artificial intelligence and data centers extract maximum benefit from this distorted capital pricing, locking in the cost of long-term financing before inflation completely destroys the currency's purchasing power11.

The cost of this systemic banquet has been entirely and inevitably shifted onto the real sector of the economy, the middle class, and ordinary consumers. The primary instrument for value extraction is the emission tax—inflation. The substitution of long-term debt with short-term bills in the US does not annul systemic imbalances; it merely redistributes the pressure onto the exchange rate. The weakening of the US dollar following the activation of the Treasury's printing press automatically exports inflation through imported prices11.

Coupled with persistently high energy prices (Brent crude reached $97 a barrel amid prolonged conflicts in the Middle East and threats to close the Strait of Hormuz)5, this guarantees that the population will pay for fiscal deficits through a permanent loss of real income. Mechanisms of direct financial repression are already hitting the most vulnerable strata: in Japan, the normalization of rates by the Bank of Japan has led to a sharp increase in interest rates on student loans to 2.922%, closely approaching the legal ceiling of 3%, instantly stripping liquidity from households6. In the US, mortgage rates have settled at 6.75%, freezing the social mobility of the population and turning the housing market into a privilege for institutional capital4. The mathematics of global debt leave systems with no other exit: saving financial leverage is possible exclusively through the organized destruction of the purchasing power of fiat currencies1.

FRAGMENTATION & PROTECTIONISM

GEOPOLITICS • DE-DOLLARIZATION
04

FRAGMENTATION & PROTECTIONISM

The global architecture of free capital movement has been completely dismantled. The monetization of Western debt, the geopolitical weaponization of the dollar, and sanctions regimes have led to an unprecedented flight of sovereign capital from developing countries into defensive assets and the accelerated construction of a parallel settlement infrastructure. The world has definitively fractured into isolated financial blocs.

Reserves and Gold demonstrate a tectonic, structural outflow of trust from the fiat system based on the dollar. Institutional demand for gold has reached historical anomalies. In 2025, global central banks purchased 863 tonnes of physical gold worth over $95 billion, and in the second quarter of 2026, the pace did not wane—289 tonnes were purchased (a 62% year-over-year increase)37. The primary driver is China. The People's Bank of China continuously increased its gold reserves for 21 consecutive months up to July 2026, bringing their volume to 2,366 tonnes, which by market valuation exceeds $306 billion and represents about 9% of the country's total gold and foreign exchange reserves ($3.41 trillion)40.

Simultaneously, China is methodically dumping American sovereign debt. The volume of US Treasury securities on China's balance sheet plummeted to $633.4 billion in June 2026—a 75% drop from peak values in 2011, when China controlled 28% of all US external debt (the share has now fallen to 7%)40. Beijing is joined by countries from India and Kazakhstan to Poland and emerging markets45. This shift is perfectly described by the concept of the "Reverse Kindleberger Trap": in the event of a systemic crisis, capital will no longer flee to the US as a "safe haven"13. Sovereigns have realized that US government bonds carry the risk of selective defaults, asset freezes, and inflationary devaluation initiated by the US Treasury itself13. The response has been a massive migration into physical gold as the only unconditional bearer asset devoid of counterparty risk.

Trade and currency blocs are rapidly crystallizing into real, functioning technological solutions, definitively destroying the hegemony of SWIFT. The multilateral mBridge project, initially supervised by the Bank for International Settlements and uniting the central banks of China, Hong Kong, Thailand, the UAE, and Saudi Arabia, has moved into a stage of full-scale commercial operation48. The platform has already processed transactions worth over $55.5 billion, with 95% of the settlement volume attributed to the digital yuan (e-CNY), marking a colossal breakthrough in the internationalization of the Chinese currency47.

Concurrently, the BRICS nations, under the chairmanship of India in 2026, are forcing the deployment of the BRICS Pay infrastructure49. The BRICS strategy does not lie in the utopian creation of a single currency or stablecoin, but in the pragmatic technological interoperability of already existing, highly developed national fast payment systems: India's UPI (already operating in 8 countries), Brazil's Pix, China's CIPS (which processes 180 trillion yuan a year), and Russia's SPFS48. The system will allow clearing in national currencies, bypassing the dollar and Western correspondent banks. Test implementation phases for QR code payments are planned throughout 2026 in Turkey, Egypt, and the UAE48.

The effect of these geoeconomic shifts is lethal for the unipolar system. The creation of a multipolar clearing architecture lowers transaction costs and completely neutralizes the effectiveness of Western sanctions50. De-dollarization has transitioned from the realm of political rhetoric to the domain of hard algorithmic data exchange protocols and the cross-border movement of Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDC)54. Global energy and commodity supply chains are increasingly being funded in local currencies, fragmenting world liquidity into closed blocs shielded by barriers of protectionism and capital controls. The global market is definitively transforming into an archipelago of independent, financially sovereign zones.

ACTIONABLE VERDICT

FORECASTS • STRATEGY
05

ACTIONABLE VERDICT

Risk has been transferred from the balance sheets of primary dealers, institutional investors, and overleveraged corporations to the balance sheets of sovereign states, central banks, and ultimate taxpayers. The primary instrument for value extraction becomes structural inflation, permanent financial repression through hidden yield curve management, and the devaluation of fiat debt. Capital should prepare for the severe fragmentation of global liquidity, the destruction of the dollar-based correspondent banking system, and the urgent need to migrate into real assets immune to counterparty risk (gold) and clearing infrastructure (BRICS Pay, mBridge) protected from Western fiscal dominance and sanctions control.
